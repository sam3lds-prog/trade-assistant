# Trade Assistant V2

V2 turns the app from an option pricer with a journal into a position intelligence system:
stock long and short, long calls and puts, one set of accounting rules, and analysis that
says how much evidence there is and how often similar setups worked before. It stays
advisory only. Nothing in V2 places, changes or cancels an order, and the Robinhood
connection stays read-only market data.

Everything V2 adds lives beside the classic app: the classic `/api` routes and screens work
as before, and the new routes are under `/v2`.

## Feature flags

Each V2 area can be switched off in `.env` without touching the rest of the app.

| Flag | Default | What it switches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FEATURE_UNIFIED_POSITIONS` | on | Positions screen and `/v2/positions` |
| `FEATURE_TREND_ENGINE_V2` | on once built | Analyze screen, `/v2/trend`, `/v2/analysis` |
| `FEATURE_PORTFOLIO_RISK` | on once built | Portfolio screen, `/v2/portfolio` |
| `FEATURE_SIGNAL_CALIBRATION` | on once built | Live pattern evidence and calibration |
| `FEATURE_OPTIONS_BACKTEST` | off | Option backtests: needs historical option prices, which no connected source provides |

Values: `1`, `true`, `yes`, `on` or `0`, `false`, `no`, `off`. A switched-off area answers
404 with a message naming the flag, and its button is hidden. `GET /v2/features` and
`GET /api/status` report the flags in force.

## Database migrations

The database is SQLite, created by `db.py`. V2 changes it through numbered migrations in
`schema_v2.py`, recorded in a `schema_migrations` table. Each runs once, inside a
transaction, when the app opens the database; a failure rolls that migration back and
stops the app rather than leaving a half-changed database.

Alembic was considered and not used: the app has no ORM, one local SQLite file per user and
no server to migrate, so a forty-line runner with plain SQL is easier to read and has no
extra dependency. Rules:

- Never edit a migration that has shipped. Add a new one.
- A migration that moves data must leave the old tables readable, so the classic screens keep working.
- `Database.schema_version()` returns the highest version applied; `/api/status` reports it.

Back up `data/trade_assistant.db` before updating if you want a way back: an older version of
the app ignores the new tables but cannot undo them.

## Unified positions (Sprint 1)

### Tables (migration 1)

- `positions`: one row per idea. `instrument_type` STOCK, OPTION (OPTION_SPREAD reserved),
  `direction` LONG or SHORT, `status` PLANNED, OPEN, PARTIALLY_CLOSED, CLOSED or CANCELLED.
  `quantity` is the planned size (shares, or contracts), `multiplier` is 1 for stock and 100
  for an equity option. The plan: `thesis`, `setup_type`, `intended_horizon` (trading days),
  `invalidation_price` (a price of the underlying stock), `target_1`, `target_2`,
  `risk_amount`, `initial_risk_per_unit`. The accounting columns (`open_quantity`,
  `average_entry_price`, `average_exit_price`, `realized_pnl`, `fees`, `opened_at`,
  `closed_at`, `status`) are a cache rebuilt from the executions after every change.
  `legacy_trade_id` links a position that mirrors a classic option-journal entry.
- `option_contracts`: the option leg of a position (type, strike, expiration, OCC symbol,
  multiplier, ratio). One leg for now; the key allows more for spreads later.
- `executions`: every fill. `side` BUY, SELL, SELL_SHORT or BUY_TO_COVER, `quantity`,
  `price` (per share; per share of the contract for options), `fees`, `executed_at`,
  `source` MANUAL, ROBINHOOD or IMPORT.
- `position_snapshots`: what the market looked like when the position was planned, entered,
  exited or reviewed: prices of the stock, SPY, QQQ and VIX; RSI, MACD, averages, ADX, ATR,
  volume ratio; swing structure, trend state, relative strength, scores and market regime;
  for options, implied volatility, IV rank, Greeks, bid, ask and days to expiration. Each
  records `signal_engine_version`, `data_timestamp`, `data_source` and `bar_status`
  (whether the last daily bar was complete), with everything else in `raw_json`.

### Accounting rules (`positions.py`)

Executions are the record; everything else is worked out from them.

- A LONG position opens with BUY and closes with SELL. A SHORT position opens with
  SELL_SHORT (or SELL) and closes with BUY_TO_COVER (or BUY).
- Average cost. Opening fills move the average cost of what is open; closing fills realize
  `sign × (exit − average cost) × quantity × multiplier`, with sign +1 long and −1 short.
- Fees are subtracted from realized profit when paid.
- A fill that would close more than is open, or close before anything is open, is refused.
  The whole sequence is checked, so a back-dated fill cannot break later ones.
- Options are whole contracts, and only long calls and puts for now.
- Status: PLANNED until the first fill, OPEN, PARTIALLY_CLOSED once some has been closed,
  CLOSED when nothing is open, CANCELLED only from PLANNED.
- Unrealized profit uses the current mark: the stock's last price, or the option's
  midpoint (model value when there is no two-sided quote; intrinsic value after expiration).
- R multiple: total profit divided by the initial risk, which is the entered dollars at
  risk; else `initial_risk_per_unit × size × multiplier`; else, for stock, the distance from
  the average entry to the invalidation price times the size; else, for a bought option, the
  premium paid.
- Times without a zone are New York time; a plain date means that day's 4 p.m. close; fills
  in the future are refused.

Example: short 1,000 CRWD at $250, cover 300 at $240 and 400 at $235. Open: 300 shares.
Realized: $3,000 + $6,000 = $9,000 before fees. At $243.60 the 300 open show $1,920
unrealized.

### The classic option journal

Migration 1 copies every classic journal entry into `positions` as a long call or put
(`setup_type` "option journal", `legacy_trade_id` set). After that, every change in the
journal (save, filled attempt, close, cancel) updates its mirror in the same transaction,
so the two never disagree. The journal stays the place to record those trades; the
Positions screen shows them and refuses fills for them.

### API

| Method and path | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v2/features` | Feature flags in force |
| `GET /v2/positions?status=open\|planned\|closed\|cancelled\|all` | Positions with marks, P&L and thesis check |
| `POST /v2/positions` | Create a position, optionally with its entry fill |
| `GET /v2/positions/{id}` | One position with legs, executions, snapshots and ledger |
| `POST /v2/positions/{id}/executions` | Record a fill |
| `POST /v2/positions/{id}/plan` | Change thesis, setup, horizon, invalidation, targets, risk, notes |
| `POST /v2/positions/{id}/cancel` | Cancel a plan that never filled |
| `POST /v2/positions/{id}/snapshot` | Take a REVIEW snapshot now |

Create body:

```json
{"symbol": "CRWD", "instrument_type": "STOCK", "direction": "SHORT", "quantity": 1000,
 "thesis": "Lower highs under resistance", "setup_type": "reversal", "intended_horizon": 15,
 "invalidation_price": 265, "target_1": 230, "target_2": 215,
 "entry": {"price": 250.0, "fees": 0, "executed_at": "2026-10-01T10:05"}}
```

For a call or put add `"instrument_type": "OPTION"` and
`"option": {"option_type": "PUT", "strike": 205, "expiration": "2026-11-06"}`; the entry
price is per share of the contract.

The response includes `ledger` (open quantity, cost basis, averages, realized, fees,
initial risk), `pnl` (realized, unrealized, total, fees, r_multiple, market_value), `mark`,
`thesis_check` (INTACT, INVALIDATED, TARGET_1_REACHED, TARGET_2_REACHED, NO_INVALIDATION,
UNKNOWN) and `snapshots`.

## Finished and unfinished bars (Sprint 2)

During market hours the last daily bar is today's bar so far. It can change shape by the
close, so nothing read from it may be shown as confirmed.

- A bar is **finished** when it was fetched after 4:10 p.m. New York time on its own date
  (the margin lets closing prints arrive). A bar fetched earlier stays unfinished, even after
  the close, until it is fetched again. `Market.bars()` marks each row in `bar_complete`.
- The cache refetches an unfinished bar once its day has closed, and every 15 minutes while
  a screen wants today's bar. `bars(..., complete=True)` leaves the unfinished bar out and
  does not refetch for it.
- Studies (pattern lab, backtest, walk-forward) use finished bars only: today's bar so far
  is never an event or an outcome. The existing no-look-ahead rules are unchanged.
- Signals carry a status: **CONFIRMED** when they come from a finished bar, **PROVISIONAL**
  when they come from today's bar so far. Recent patterns, the indicator readings and the
  swing structure say which; when the last bar is unfinished, the chart draws it dashed and
  the structure is also given on finished bars alone (`confirmed_structure`).
- Each cached history records how its source adjusts prices (`bars_meta.adjustment`,
  migration 2): `none` for demo prices, `split`, `split_dividend`, or `unknown` when the
  source does not say. Tradier and Robinhood do not state it, so they are `unknown`, and the
  data checks look for one-day jumps that suggest an unadjusted split.

`GET /v2/data/{symbol}` returns the data checks: the last bar's status, when the bars were
fetched, their adjustment, and any of these findings: `PARTIAL_BAR`, `STALE` (the last
finished bar is older than the last close), `NON_POSITIVE`, `OHLC_INCONSISTENT`,
`ZERO_VOLUME`, `GAPS` (three or more weekdays with no bar), `SUSPECT_SPLIT`,
`SHORT_HISTORY` (under 260 finished bars), `ADJUSTMENT`. Each has a level (`info`, `warn`,
`fail`); the report's `status` is the worst of them. The Research screen shows them.

## Trend Engine V2 (Sprint 3)

`trend.py` gives each day one of seven states (STRONG_UP, UP, UP_WEAKENING, NEUTRAL,
DOWN_DEVELOPING, DOWN, STRONG_DOWN), the evidence behind it and how settled it is. Every
input uses only that day's bar and earlier ones; a test checks that the states for the first
k days are the same whether or not later days exist.

Evidence, with the points each kind can give to the bullish or the bearish side:

| Kind | Points | Readings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swing structure | 22 | latest swing high and low labels (HH/HL, LH/LL), and breaks of the last higher low or lower high |
| Moving averages | 22 | close against the 20-day (4), 50-day (5) and 200-day (6) averages; 50-day against 200-day (4); slope of the 50-day (3) |
| Momentum | 20 | MACD against its signal (5); histogram direction (3); MACD against zero (4); RSI 14 (4); 20-day change (4) |
| Relative strength | 16 | return less SPY's over three months (6) and one month (4); less the sector fund's over three months (6) |
| Volume | 12 | volume on up days against down days over 20 days (7); on-balance volume over 20 days (5) |
| Validated patterns | 8 | patterns that fired in the last five days and passed the pattern lab |

Graded readings (RSI, slopes, relative strength, volume) earn points in proportion up to a
full-scale value. The bullish and bearish scores are the points each side won as a share of
the points that could be scored that day (0 to 100 each); readings without data (no sector
fund, no lab run, too little history) are left out and lower `coverage`. **Scores are
evidence points, not probabilities.** RSI above 70 is not counted as bearish: in a trend,
overbought is more often strength than a top.

State machine on the net score (bullish less bearish): a state is entered at 25 and kept
down to 15; strong states need 55, the swing structure and the order of price, 50-day and
200-day to agree. An uptrend never jumps to DOWN: it goes through UP_WEAKENING (net below 15
or the uptrend structure broken) or DOWN_DEVELOPING (net at −25 or below). DOWN_DEVELOPING
becomes DOWN once the swing structure turns down or after five days. A downtrend passes
through NEUTRAL before UP. Below half coverage the state is NEUTRAL.

Confirmation level: EARLY (under three days in the state), DEVELOPING (under ten),
ESTABLISHED. Confidence (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW) rises with coverage, a clear net score, ADX of
25 or more and time in the state, and falls with balanced evidence, ADX under 18, daily
ranges in the top 15% of the past year, and an unfinished bar; the reasons are listed.

When today's bar is unfinished the response has both `provisional` (with today's bar so
far) and `confirmed` (the last finished bar), and `changed_today` says whether they differ.
Changes of state are listed for finished bars only.

Relative strength uses SPY, QQQ (shown, not scored) and the stock's Select Sector SPDR fund
from a fixed list of about 270 large companies (`sectors.py`), or from the SEC industry code
when SEC lookups are on.

API: `GET /v2/trend/{symbol}` and `GET /v2/analysis/{symbol}` (trend, data checks, quote,
and the sections later sprints add). Position snapshots now record the trend state,
bullish and bearish scores and relative strength. Screen: **Analyze a stock**.

## Market regime (Sprint 4)

`regime.py` names what the market is doing each day, from SPY's trend state (the trend
engine run on SPY), QQQ's as a cross-check, and volatility:

| Regime | When |
| --- | --- |
| BULL_CALM | SPY uptrend or strong uptrend; volatility calm or normal |
| BULL_VOLATILE | SPY uptrend; volatility elevated or stressed |
| RANGE | SPY without a trend |
| TRANSITION | SPY uptrend weakening or downtrend developing, or SPY and QQQ in opposite trends |
| BEAR_CALM | SPY downtrend; volatility calm or normal |
| BEAR_VOLATILE | SPY downtrend; volatility elevated or stressed |

Volatility: the VIX close when the source has VIX history (under 15 calm, 15 to 20 normal,
20 to 30 elevated, above 30 stressed); otherwise SPY's 20-day realized volatility ranked
against the past year (under 30% of days calm, under 70% normal, under 90% elevated,
otherwise stressed; 35% annualized is always stressed). History that does not look like VIX
is ignored. The demo source has no VIX. Breadth is not included. The regime is causal, like
the trend states, and has a provisional reading when SPY's bar is unfinished.

Indicators in context (`regime.interpret`): RSI, the 52-week range position and the
Bollinger bands are read for the stock's own trend state and the regime. Overbought in an
uptrend reads as strength, in a range as stretched, in a downtrend as a rally that tends to
fade; near the 52-week high while the market falls reads as relative strength; above the
upper band in an uptrend reads as walking the band; squeezed bands say a bigger move often
follows, not which way. These readings explain; they add nothing to the trend scores.

API: `GET /v2/regime`; `/v2/analysis/{symbol}` adds `regime` and `interpretation`. Position
snapshots record `market_regime`.

## Signal history and calibration (Sprint 5)

`signalbook.py` keeps every past signal in `signal_occurrences` (migration 3): each pattern
completing and each change of trend state, on finished bars only, with the trend state, net
score and market regime that day and what followed (returns 5, 10 and 20 trading days on,
SPY over the same days, and the best and worst prices in the next 20 days). Outcomes are
measured as in the pattern lab: entry at the next day's open, exit at the close h days
after the signal. Rebuilding replaces a stock's rows.

`POST /v2/signals/history/build` (`{"symbols": [...], "years": 5}`, default the
watchlist) builds it; the Pattern lab screen has a button. The trend states used for it
leave out the validated-pattern points, because the lab found those verdicts on the same
history.

`GET /v2/signals/{symbol}` lists what fired on the stock in the last ten bars (patterns and
changes of trend state, each CONFIRMED or PROVISIONAL) and, for each, how its past cases
turned out over 10 days: across all stocks, in the current market regime, and on this stock
alone, as "went the signal's way in X% of N cases" with a 95% Wilson interval, average
return, return against SPY and best and worst moves. Fewer than 30 cases are flagged.
Overlapping cases are not independent; the number of separate periods is given.

Calibration: every day's net score (without pattern points) is put in a range (−100 to −50,
−50 to −25, −25 to 0, 0 to 25, 25 to 50, 50 to 100) and the share of days followed by a
gain over 10 and 20 days is counted, also by regime. The score is called **calibrated**
only when the ranges with at least 30 days rise in order (within two points) and the
highest range beat the lowest significantly (chi-squared, p < 0.05). Otherwise it is
reported as not calibrated and must be read as a count of evidence only.
`GET /v2/signals/calibration` returns the table; `GET /v2/patterns/{symbol}` gives each
pattern's events on the stock, its record there and across all stocks, and the lab verdict.

Evidence score and historical frequency are always shown separately. Neither is a forecast.

## Position evaluator and trade grades (Sprint 6)

`POST /v2/position/evaluate` grades a stock trade, long or short:

```json
{"symbol": "NVDA", "direction": "SHORT", "entry_price": 694.5, "invalidation_price": 738,
 "target_1": 580, "target_2": 556, "horizon_days": 20}
```

Every field but the symbol is optional: the entry defaults to the current price; the
invalidation to a quarter of a daily range (ATR) beyond the last swing low (long) or high
(short), or two ranges away when no swing is within six; targets to the first two support
or resistance levels in the way at least half the risk away, else two and three times the
risk. With `position_id` an existing stock position is graded with its own plan and average
cost.

| Component | Weight | Scored from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trend alignment | 30 | the trend state for this direction; less for a provisional or low-confidence reading |
| Market regime | 15 | how the regime favours longs or shorts |
| Reward to risk | 20 | target 1 against the invalidation: 1:1 scores 40, 2:1 80, 3:1 100 |
| Past cases | 15 | the score range's frequency, only if the score is calibrated; recent signals' records each way |
| Volatility and fit | 10 | stop distance in ATRs (under 0.5 or over 4 is penalised), unusual volatility, dollar volume |
| Catalysts | 10 | earnings within the holding period, within two trading days, or unknown |

Grade: A 80+, B 65+, C 50+, D 35+, else AVOID. Caps: AVOID for an invalidation or target on
the wrong side, target 1 under 0.75 times the risk, or data that failed its checks; D for a
short against a strong uptrend or a long against a strong downtrend; C for earnings within
two trading days. The response gives the levels (and whether they were yours or
suggested), reward to risk, a size that keeps the loss at the invalidation within
`MAX_RISK_PER_TRADE_PCT` of `ACCOUNT_SIZE`, each component with its reasons, analogs (past
changes into the same trend state), and what would improve the grade.

Shorts also get `short_sale_risk`: borrow availability, fees and short interest cannot be
seen by this app and are flagged as unknown; a run-up of more than 30% in a month (worse on
heavy volume), opening gaps of more than 1.5 ATRs in the past year, an uptrend against the
short and earnings within a month are flagged from the data.

The grade summarises the checks. It is not a probability.

## Options contract evaluator (Sprint 7)

`tradeassist/options/evaluator.py` finds the calls (bullish) or puts (bearish) that fit a
thesis about the stock. `POST /v2/options/evaluate`:

```json
{"symbol": "NVDA", "direction": "BULLISH", "target_price": 780, "invalidation_price": 650,
 "horizon_days": 20, "contracts": 1, "budget": 1000}
```

Target and invalidation default to the stock evaluator's suggestions. Expirations: the first
at least a week after the horizon date and those nearest two, three and four and a half times
the horizon (up to four), or the ones given in `expirations`. Candidates: delta between 0.10
and 0.90, nearest the stock price, up to 24 per expiration.

Each contract is valued with the simulator's Black-76 model in a scenario matrix: the stock
at the invalidation price, unchanged, at target 1 and at target 2; on the horizon date and
halfway there; implied volatility −5, 0 and +5 points. Entry at the midpoint; the spread
cost is shown apart. "If invalidated" is the worse of the two dates at the invalidation
price, never more than the premium. Also: return on premium at target, reward to risk,
breakevens at the horizon and at expiration, and the market-implied chance of profit at
expiration.

Rankings: **BEST THESIS MATCH** (largest return on premium at target 1 by the horizon),
**LOWEST COST** (cheapest that still makes money there), **BEST RISK-ADJUSTED** (most gained
at target per dollar lost if invalidated), **MOST CONSERVATIVE** (smallest share of the
premium lost if invalidated, with at least twice the horizon to expiration).

Avoid list, with reasons: no two-sided quote, spread over 10% of the midpoint, open interest
under 100, expiring before the horizon, losing money even at the target, over budget. Flags:
earnings before expiration (volatility usually falls after the report), paying at the target
only if volatility holds, needing the stock past the target to break even.

The exit rule is on the stock: exit when it closes through the invalidation price, whatever
the option is quoted at. The response also has the stock trade's own grade and the
market-implied chances of the stock being past the target and the invalidation price on the
horizon date (option-implied, not forecasts).

`POST /v2/options/compare` takes the same thesis plus `contracts_to_compare`
(`[{"option_type": "CALL", "strike": 760, "expiration": "2026-11-13"}]`, up to eight) and
values just those. Only bought calls and puts are evaluated. On the Analyze screen each pick
can be opened in the simulator or saved as a planned position with the thesis's levels.

## Portfolio risk (Sprint 8)

`GET /v2/portfolio/risk` adds up every open position (option-journal trades included):

- **Exposure** in stock dollars: shares times price (negative short); delta × 100 ×
  contracts × stock price for options. Gross, net, long and short totals, by stock and by
  sector (the sector funds of `sectors.py`).
- **SPY-equivalent dollars**: each exposure times its beta to SPY from the last six months
  of daily returns on finished bars.
- **Greeks**: theta per day, vega per volatility point, and the change in dollar delta for
  a 1% move (gamma), for the options.
- **Risk to invalidation**: the loss if each stock went to its invalidation price now
  (option repriced with the model there, never more than its value); unknown for stock
  without an invalidation price, the whole value for an option without one. Compared with
  `MAX_OPEN_RISK_PCT` of `ACCOUNT_SIZE`.
- **A bad day**: 1.645 standard deviations of daily P&L from the six-month covariance of
  returns and the exposures (deltas only; assumes the past six months are typical).
- **Correlation** of the holdings' daily returns; pairs above 0.8 held the same way are
  flagged as closer to one bet than two.
- **Stress tests**: market −5%/−10%/−20% with volatility +5/+10/+20 points, market
  +5%/+10% with volatility −2/−4, volatility +10 with the market flat, and the largest
  position gapping 20% against you. Stocks move their beta times the market; options are
  repriced with the simulator's model. `POST /v2/portfolio/stress` runs your own
  (`{"scenarios": [{"name": "...", "market_move_pct": -15, "iv_shift_points": 12,
  "moves": {"NVDA": -25}}]}`).
- **Warnings**: over the open-risk limit, one stock over 25% of the account, one sector over
  40% of gross exposure, positions through their invalidation price, options within seven
  days of expiration, time decay over 0.2% of the account a day, missing prices.

**Daily loss** (`DAILY_LOSS_MODE`): `REALIZED_PLUS_UNREALIZED` (default) counts what was
closed today plus today's change in open positions (from the previous close, or from the
fill for positions opened today); `REALIZED_ONLY` counts closed trades only. WARN at three
quarters of `DAILY_LOSS_STOP_PCT`, STOP at it. With portfolio risk on, the classic ticket's
daily loss check uses this figure too, so a large open loss stops new trades before it is
realised. Screen: **Portfolio risk**.

## Journal analytics (Sprint 9)

`GET /v2/journal/stats` covers every closed unified position (stock long and short, calls
and puts, and option-journal trades through their mirrors):

- Totals: trades, wins and losses, win rate, average win and loss, **profit factor** (gross
  wins over gross losses; none when there are no losses), **expectancy** per trade in
  dollars and in R (trades without a risk are left out of R), total, largest win and loss,
  longest losing streak, average days held, equity curve of closed results and its deepest
  drawdown.
- **MAE and MFE**: the worst and best the stock moved against and for each trade while it
  was held, from daily highs and lows after the entry day to the exit day, in percent and in
  R (the distance from entry to the invalidation price). Calls and puts are measured on the
  stock, because option prices have no daily history here.
- Breakdowns: by setup, by bet on a rise or a fall, stock or options, trend state and
  market regime at entry (from the ENTRY snapshot), and holding time.
- Habits, each with results with and without it: held after the stock closed through the
  invalidation price, exited before target 1 without being invalidated, added at a worse
  price, risk above the per-trade limit, traded against the trend at entry, overrode a
  ticket limit (option journal).
- Under 20 closed trades every figure is flagged as unreliable.

`GET /v2/positions/{id}/analytics`: one position's MAE and MFE so far, its habits, and its
entry snapshot beside the latest exit or review snapshot.

Fills recorded after the fact now get their ENTRY and EXIT snapshots **as of the fill date**:
indicators, swing structure, trend state, regime and relative strength from the bars up to
that day's close (the engines are causal, so this is what they would have said then). Option
quotes and Greeks have no history here and are left out of such snapshots.

The Journal screen shows these analytics below the option journal; the Positions screen
shows each position's own.

## Versions of the logic

Results that come from an engine carry its version (`versions.py`), and snapshots store
them, so a result can be traced to the logic that produced it after the logic changes.
