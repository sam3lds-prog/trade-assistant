# Trade Assistant V2

V2 turns the app from an option pricer with a journal into a position intelligence system:
stock long and short, long calls and puts, one set of accounting rules, and analysis that
says how much evidence there is and how often similar setups worked before. It stays
advisory only. Nothing in V2 places, changes or cancels an order, and the Robinhood
connection stays read-only: market data, plus your past orders and holdings only when you
switch that on (2.6, `ROBINHOOD_ACCOUNT_READ=1`).

Everything V2 adds lives beside the classic app: the classic `/api` routes and screens work
as before, and the new routes are under `/v2`.

## Database migrations

The database is SQLite, created by `db.py`. V2 changes it through numbered migrations in
`schema_v2.py`, recorded in a `schema_migrations` table. Each runs once, inside a
transaction, when the app opens the database; a failure rolls that migration back and
stops the app rather than leaving a half-changed database.

Alembic was considered and not used: the app has no ORM, one local SQLite file per user and
no server to migrate, so a forty-line runner with plain SQL is easier to read and has no
extra dependency. Rules:

- Never edit a migration that has shipped. Add a new one.
- A migration that moves data must leave the old tables readable, so the classic screens keep working.
- `Database.schema_version()` returns the highest version applied; `/api/status` reports it.

Since 2.1.1 the app copies the database to `data/backups/` before it applies any pending
migration (`backup.py`; SQLite's online backup, so the copy is consistent; the newest ten
are kept). An older version of the app ignores the new tables but cannot undo them, so the
copy is the way back. Migrations are tested on databases made by released versions 0.4.0,
2.0.0 and 2.1.0 (`tests/fixtures/`, `tests/test_migrations.py`).

| Version | Name | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | unified positions | `positions`, `option_contracts`, `executions`, `position_snapshots`; journal trades mirrored |
| 2 | bar adjustment | how cached daily bars are adjusted for splits and dividends |
| 3, 4 | signal occurrences | each past signal with its context and what followed; the day's range and the lowest and highest close over each horizon |
| 5 | snapshot provenance and audited corrections (2.1.1) | snapshot source and timing columns, one effective snapshot per lifecycle event, `execution_corrections`, `executions.external_id` (unique per source), `snapshot_reconstruction` |
| 6 | adjustments and option events (2.2) | `position_adjustments` (borrow fees, dividends, cash, splits), `option_events` |
| 7 | research runs (2.3) | `research_runs` |
| 8 | database rules and a log of repairs (2.3) | the triggers below, `maintenance_log` |
| 9 | one database rule per option-leg field (2.3.1) | replaces migration 8's combined option-leg rule |
| 10 | AI requests (2.4) | `ai_usage`, for the daily limit and the health screen |
| 11 | broker history (2.6) | `broker_imports`, `broker_activity`: your imported trade rows, each with its original columns |
| 12 | import links (2.6) | `broker_import_rows`: every import a row came in, so removing one keeps rows another also has |

### Database rules (migration 8)

SQLite cannot add CHECK constraints to an existing table, so these are triggers that refuse
an insert or update with a plain message (the API turns it into a 409 that says nothing was
saved):

- a position's planned quantity and multiplier are above zero;
- an option leg's strike and multiplier are above zero and its expiration is a date (one rule
  per field since migration 9, so a leg with one old fault can still not be given another);
- a fill's time is a full timestamp (a fill's quantity above zero, and its price and fees at
  or above zero, were already enforced by migration 1);
- a split's ratio is above zero.

Existing rows that would break a rule are not changed; the reconciliation report lists them
under `rule_breaks`. An update is refused only when it makes a good value bad, so a row from
an older version can still have its other figures recomputed. Foreign keys are on for every
connection.

## Unified positions (Sprint 1)

### Tables (migration 1)

- `positions`: one row per idea. `instrument_type` STOCK, OPTION (OPTION_SPREAD reserved),
  `direction` LONG or SHORT, `status` PLANNED, OPEN, PARTIALLY_CLOSED, CLOSED or CANCELLED.
  `quantity` is the planned size (shares, or contracts), `multiplier` is 1 for stock and 100
  for an equity option. The plan: `thesis`, `setup_type`, `intended_horizon` (trading days),
  `invalidation_price` (a price of the underlying stock), `target_1`, `target_2`,
  `risk_amount`, `initial_risk_per_unit`. The accounting columns (`open_quantity`,
  `average_entry_price`, `average_exit_price`, `realized_pnl`, `fees`, `opened_at`,
  `closed_at`, `status`) are a cache rebuilt from the executions after every change.
  `legacy_trade_id` links a position that mirrors a classic option-journal entry.
- `option_contracts`: the option leg of a position (type, strike, expiration, OCC symbol,
  multiplier, ratio). One leg for now; the key allows more for spreads later.
- `executions`: every fill. `side` BUY, SELL, SELL_SHORT or BUY_TO_COVER, `quantity`,
  `price` (per share; per share of the contract for options), `fees`, `executed_at`,
  `source` MANUAL, ROBINHOOD or IMPORT.
- `position_snapshots`: what the market looked like when the position was planned, entered,
  exited or reviewed: prices of the stock, SPY, QQQ and VIX; RSI, MACD, averages, ADX, ATR,
  volume ratio; swing structure, trend state, relative strength, scores and market regime;
  for options, implied volatility, IV rank, Greeks, bid, ask and days to expiration. Each
  records `signal_engine_version`, `data_timestamp`, `data_source` and `bar_status`
  (whether the last daily bar was complete), with everything else in `raw_json`.

### Accounting rules (`positions.py`)

Executions are the record; everything else is worked out from them.

- A LONG position opens with BUY and closes with SELL. A SHORT position opens with
  SELL_SHORT (or SELL) and closes with BUY_TO_COVER (or BUY).
- A remainder smaller than the smallest fill that can be recorded (a millionth of a share),
  left by rounding or a split, is closed at the last sale's price once nothing follows it, and
  a sale up to that much larger than what is open closes what is open (2.3.1, `rounded_off`).
- Average cost. Opening fills move the average cost of what is open; closing fills realize
  `sign × (exit − average cost) × quantity × multiplier`, with sign +1 long and −1 short.
- Fees are subtracted from realized profit when paid.
- A fill that would close more than is open, or close before anything is open, is refused.
  The whole sequence is checked, so a back-dated fill cannot break later ones.
- Options are whole contracts, and only long calls and puts for now.
- Status: PLANNED until the first fill, OPEN, PARTIALLY_CLOSED once some has been closed,
  CLOSED when nothing is open, CANCELLED only from PLANNED. The only changes allowed are
  PLANNED → OPEN, OPEN → PARTIALLY_CLOSED, PARTIALLY_CLOSED → CLOSED, OPEN → CLOSED and
  PLANNED → CANCELLED (2.1.1, `positions.TRANSITIONS`).
- **A closed position is finished** (2.1.1). It takes no more fills, buying or selling: the
  answer is "This position is already closed. Create a new position for a new trade." A new
  trade in the same stock is a new position with its own id, so the old one keeps its fills,
  snapshots and profit. A back-dated fill that would close the position completely before a
  later fill is refused for the same reason. Versions before 2.1.1 allowed a position to be
  bought again after closing; such positions are kept exactly as recorded, still add up their
  fills, and are listed in `ledger.lifecycle_issues` (REOPENED_AFTER_CLOSE) and on the
  position's screen. Mistakes in a finished position's history are fixed through audited
  corrections, never by adding fills.
- **Every number is checked** (2.1.1): quantities, prices, fees, plan levels, risk amounts,
  strikes and contract multipliers must be finite; NaN and infinity are refused in the API
  and again in the accounting code, and a result too large to represent is refused rather
  than stored. Zero is a valid price (an option that expired worthless closes at 0), and the
  smallest quantity recorded is 0.000001. Settings such as `ACCOUNT_SIZE` must be finite and
  above zero.
- Unrealized profit uses the current mark: the stock's last price, or the option's
  midpoint (model value when there is no two-sided quote; intrinsic value after expiration).
- R multiple: total profit divided by the initial risk, which is the entered dollars at
  risk; else `initial_risk_per_unit × size × multiplier`; else, for stock, the distance from
  the average entry to the invalidation price times the size; else, for a bought option, the
  premium paid.
- Times without a zone are New York time; a plain date means that day's 4 p.m. close; fills
  in the future are refused.

Example: short 1,000 CRWD at $250, cover 300 at $240 and 400 at $235. Open: 300 shares.
Realized: $3,000 + $6,000 = $9,000 before fees. At $243.60 the 300 open show $1,920
unrealized.

### The classic option journal

Migration 1 copies every classic journal entry into `positions` as a long call or put
(`setup_type` "option journal", `legacy_trade_id` set). After that, every change in the
journal (save, filled attempt, close, cancel) updates its mirror in the same transaction,
so the two never disagree. The journal stays the place to record those trades; the
Positions screen shows them and refuses fills and corrections for them.

Since 2.1.1 the mirror's fills are reconciled with the journal rather than deleted and
inserted again, so their ids stay the same and anything tied to them (snapshots,
corrections) stays valid; running the sync any number of times changes nothing once the two
agree. If a journal entry's prices or times differ from its mirror's fills, the fill is
updated in place and the change is recorded as a `JOURNAL_SYNC` correction. Mirrors get the
same lifecycle snapshots as other positions (below): the journal tells the position desk
after each change, and the PLAN snapshot is the quote the ticket saved when it was planned.
Journal times are when the fill was logged in the app (`entry_at`, `exit_at`), which is the
closest record the journal has of the fill time.

### Lifecycle snapshots (2.1.1, `snapshots.py`)

| Snapshot | When | Tied to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PLAN | a position saved without a fill; a journal ticket's saved quote | nothing |
| ENTRY | the first opening fill | that fill |
| EXIT | the position closes | the last closing fill |
| REVIEW | when you ask for one | nothing |

Each snapshot records how it was made:

| Field | Values |
| --- | --- |
| `method` | `LIVE` (market data at that moment), `PRIOR_BAR` (rebuilt from the last daily bar that had finished before the fill), `END_OF_DAY` (rebuilt from the fill day's close, as 2.0 and 2.1 did), `JOURNAL_QUOTE` |
| `capture_timing` | `CONTEMPORANEOUS` (taken at the time) or `RETROSPECTIVE` (rebuilt later) |
| `executed_at`, `execution_id` | the fill it is about |
| `observation_cutoff` | the latest moment of market data it uses |
| `captured_at` | when it was made |
| `uses_later_data` | true when the data is from after the fill: hindsight |
| `provenance` | where it came from (`desk`, `reconcile`, `option journal ticket`, or `before 2.1.1` with `inferred`) |

**Time accuracy.** A fill recorded within 15 minutes of when it happened gets a LIVE
snapshot. A fill recorded later gets a PRIOR_BAR one: indicators, swing structure, trend,
regime and relative strength as of the last daily bar that had finished before the fill, so
a 10 a.m. entry is read from the previous day's close, never from that day's 4 p.m. close;
a fill after 4 p.m. may use that day's bar. Daily bars are the only history the app has, and
it uses no intraday data. Option quotes and Greeks have no history here and are left out of
rebuilt snapshots; the stock price in a rebuilt stock ENTRY or EXIT is the fill price itself.
Journal analytics use an ENTRY snapshot as "what was known at entry" only when it does not
use later data (`analytics.entry_context`); others count as "not recorded".

**One effective snapshot.** Each position has at most one effective PLAN, ENTRY and EXIT
(`superseded_at` null; a unique index enforces it). A snapshot is superseded, never deleted,
when its fill's time is corrected, when the position is no longer closed (EXIT), or when a
hindsight snapshot is replaced by a point-in-time one. Migration 5 inferred the provenance
of every snapshot taken before 2.1.1 and marked any second ENTRY or EXIT superseded.

**Backfill.** `POST /v2/snapshots/backfill` (`{"dry_run": false, "upgrade_hindsight": true}`;
the Positions screen's "Rebuild missing snapshots" button) adds the ENTRY, EXIT and PLAN
snapshots each position should have and can be rebuilt, and replaces hindsight ones. It
never changes a fill, never creates a duplicate, and records each attempt in
`snapshot_reconstruction` (CREATED, or UNAVAILABLE with the reason, such as "the journal
ticket saved no quote"). Running it again finds nothing new to do. `GET /v2/snapshots/status`
lists what is missing, what still uses hindsight, and what could not be rebuilt. Market data
is fetched outside any database transaction; each write is a short transaction that checks
again that the snapshot is still needed.

### Corrections (2.1.1)

A fill entered wrongly is fixed with `POST /v2/positions/{id}/executions/{execution_id}/correct`
(`{"price": 211.5, "reason": "Broker confirmation shows 211.50"}`; any of `side`,
`quantity`, `price`, `fees`, `executed_at`, and a required `reason`), or the "Correct" button
beside each fill. The fill is changed in place and, in the same transaction, the change is
recorded in `execution_corrections`: the values before and after, the reason, the source
(`USER`, `JOURNAL_SYNC`, `REPAIR`), the time and the position's figures before and after.
The whole history must still account afterwards, or nothing is written. A correction may
change the status, including opening a closed position again when the fix shows it was
never fully closed: that is the only way a finished position changes. Snapshots tied to a
fill whose time changed, or an EXIT of a position that is no longer closed, are superseded
and rebuilt. `GET /v2/positions/{id}/corrections` and the position's `corrections` list
them. Journal mirrors are corrected through the journal.

### Imported fills (2.1.1)

`POST /v2/positions/{id}/executions` accepts `source` (`MANUAL`, `IMPORT`, `ROBINHOOD`) and
`external_id`, the broker's id for the fill. The same id from the same source is recorded
once: sending it again changes nothing, and sending it with different details is refused.
Fills without an id are never merged, because two real fills can have the same size, price
and time. There is no automatic broker import in the app; this is what one would use.

### Short positions: borrow fees, dividends and splits (2.2)

Money that moves outside the fills is recorded on the position
(`POST /v2/positions/{id}/adjustments`; the "Borrow fees, dividends and other cash" form on a
stock position), never estimated:

| Kind | For | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BORROW_FEE` | stock sold short | the fee charged, negative; optionally the yearly `rate_pct` and the days it covers (`period_start`, `period_end`) |
| `BORROW_RATE` | stock sold short | no money: the rate from a date on, used only for a labelled estimate |
| `DIVIDEND_OWED` | stock sold short | a dividend you paid, negative; or `per_share` and `quantity` |
| `DIVIDEND_RECEIVED` | stock held long | a dividend received, positive |
| `CASH_ADJUSTMENT` | any position | anything else the broker booked against it, either sign |
| `SPLIT` | stock | `ratio` new shares per old (2 for 2-for-1, 0.1 for 1-for-10) |

A position's `pnl` keeps **trading profit** (`trading_total`: what the fills made, after their
fees) apart from **financing** (`financing`: the entries above) and adds them in
`net_economic`. For a short, `financing.borrow_status` says whether borrow fees are recorded
for every night the shares were borrowed: UNKNOWN when none are, PARTIAL when any night from
the short sale to the cover (or to today, whose night is not owed yet) is not inside a
recorded fee's period (since 2.3.1; a fee without a period covers only its own date; weekends
count), RECORDED otherwise, including a short covered the same day. A missing fee is never
filled in: with a rate entered, `borrow_estimate` shows what the uncovered nights might cost,
labelled as an estimate and left out of every profit figure. An open short without full borrow records raises a BORROW_UNKNOWN
alert. Entries with a broker id (`external_id`) are imported once. A mistaken entry is voided
with a reason (`POST .../adjustments/{adjustment_id}/void`), never deleted.

A split does not change any recorded fill: fills before its date are replayed in post-split
units (r times the shares at 1/r of the price), the plan's invalidation and targets are
rescaled, and the old levels are kept in the entry's `details`. A split entered as a plain
date is the ex-date: every fill on that date is already in post-split units (2.3.1). The
planned size is rescaled when the plan or the first fill came before the split. Voiding a
split undoes its own rescaling of today's levels and size, so later splits and plan edits are
kept (a level set after a mistaken split is taken to be in its units, and is converted back
too).

A split, a voided split or a contract adjustment that would leave shares or contracts open on
a **closed** position, or make a position close completely before a later fill opens it again
(two trades in one), is refused (2.3.1): usually a fill was entered in the other units, and
the fix is to correct it. If the position really was not closed, send the request
again with `"confirm_reopen": true` and a reason (the split's `note`, the void's `reason`): the
reopening is recorded as an audited correction. Options on the stock need their own contract adjustment (below). Other corporate
actions (mergers, spin-offs, special dividends) are recorded as cash adjustments; the app
does not model them.

### How a bought option ends (2.2)

An option's `lifecycle.state` is OPEN, CLOSED (sold), EXPIRED_WORTHLESS, EXERCISED, or
**UNRESOLVED** when it is past expiration with contracts still open and no outcome recorded.
An unresolved option is never assumed worthless: its risk is unknown in the portfolio and an
UNRESOLVED_EXPIRATION alert asks for the outcome.

- **Expired worthless** (`POST /v2/positions/{id}/option/expire`): the open contracts close at
  $0 at the 4 p.m. close on the expiration date; refused before then. The stock price at
  expiration is the one you enter or the finished daily close on that date. If the option was
  in the money by a cent or more, brokers normally exercise it automatically, so recording it
  as worthless needs a reason; so does recording it with no stock price known (2.3.1).
- **Exercised** (`POST .../option/exercise`, early or at expiration): the contracts close at
  their intrinsic value against the stock price at exercise, and the shares (contracts x
  multiplier) are recorded at that same price, bought for a call and sold for a put, on a new
  stock position or on `apply_to_position_id`. Together that is buying (or selling) the shares
  at the strike after paying the premium, with each side's profit shown where it belongs.
  Refused when the option is out of the money, after expiration or before it was bought. The
  stock position lists where its shares came from (`derived_from`).
- **Contract adjusted** (`POST .../option/adjust`) after a split or other corporate action:
  new strike, new multiplier and contracts per old contract. The original terms stay in the
  event; earlier fills are replayed in the new units, so value is preserved. A plain date is
  the ex-date: that day's fills are already in the new units (2.3.1).
- **Assignment** does not apply: only bought calls and puts can be recorded. Adjusted
  contracts that deliver cash or other securities are not modelled.

Classic journal trades end in the journal (close at 0 for worthless).

### Changing the plan

`POST /v2/positions/{id}/plan` changes only the fields sent. For a position mirrored from the
option journal, thesis and notes are changed in the journal, which is their record (2.3.1). Since 2.1.1 a field sent as
`null` is cleared (`invalidation_price`, `target_1`, `target_2`, `risk_amount`,
`initial_risk_per_unit`, `intended_horizon`), a field left out keeps its value, and thesis,
setup and notes take an empty string to clear (`null` is refused). Initial risk and R
multiples follow whatever remains: with the risk amount cleared, R is measured from the
entry to the invalidation price.

## Finished and unfinished bars (Sprint 2)

During market hours the last daily bar is today's bar so far. It can change shape by the
close, so nothing read from it may be shown as confirmed.

- A bar is **finished** when it was fetched after 4:10 p.m. New York time on its own date
  (the margin lets closing prints arrive). A bar fetched earlier stays unfinished, even after
  the close, until it is fetched again. `Market.bars()` marks each row in `bar_complete`.
- The cache refetches an unfinished bar once its day has closed, and every 15 minutes while
  a screen wants today's bar. `bars(..., complete=True)` leaves the unfinished bar out and
  does not refetch for it.
- Studies (pattern lab, backtest, walk-forward) use finished bars only: today's bar so far
  is never an event or an outcome. The existing no-look-ahead rules are unchanged.
- Signals carry a status: **CONFIRMED** when they come from a finished bar, **PROVISIONAL**
  when they come from today's bar so far. Recent patterns, the indicator readings and the
  swing structure say which; when the last bar is unfinished, the chart draws it dashed and
  the structure is also given on finished bars alone (`confirmed_structure`).
- Each cached history records how its source adjusts prices (`bars_meta.adjustment`,
  migration 2): `none` for demo prices, `split`, `split_dividend`, or `unknown` when the
  source does not say. Tradier and Robinhood do not state it, so they are `unknown`, and the
  data checks look for one-day jumps that suggest an unadjusted split.

`GET /v2/data/{symbol}` returns the data checks: the last bar's status, when the bars were
fetched, their adjustment, and any of these findings: `PARTIAL_BAR`, `STALE` (the last
finished bar is older than the last close), `NON_POSITIVE`, `OHLC_INCONSISTENT`,
`ZERO_VOLUME`, `GAPS` (three or more weekdays with no bar), `SUSPECT_SPLIT`,
`SHORT_HISTORY` (under 260 finished bars), `ADJUSTMENT`. Each has a level (`info`, `warn`,
`fail`); the report's `status` is the worst of them. The Research screen shows them.

## Data freshness and failures (2.2, `freshness.py`)

Every important price carries where and when it came from: the instrument, the source, the
time the source gave for the price (`observed_at`), when the app received it
(`retrieved_at`), whether the market was open, and:

| `freshness` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| FRESH | recent enough while the market is open: 120 s for a stock quote, 300 s for an option or an index (`FRESHNESS_*_SECONDS` in `.env`) |
| DELAYED | from a source that is delayed by design (the Tradier sandbox, 15 minutes); stale after 20 |
| LAST_SESSION | the market is closed and this is the latest session's price: old but current. One extra day is allowed for a market holiday, which is not in a calendar here |
| STALE | older than allowed, or a saved copy used because the source failed |
| UNKNOWN | the source gave no time for it |

`completeness` (COMPLETE, PARTIAL, MISSING) and `warnings` cover missing bids or asks,
crossed quotes, invalid values and clock problems. Daily bars are checked against the latest
finished session.

Since 2.3.1 a quote's time is the older of its bid and ask times (what a mark and a fill depend
on), and the last trade's time only when the source gives neither, so an option with a live
quote that has not traded for days is not called stale and an old bid and ask is not made
fresh by a new trade. A time more than two minutes in the future is UNKNOWN, not fresh. The
holiday allowance reaches back to the previous weekday's session, so Friday's close is not
stale on a Monday holiday.

Failures: Tradier requests already retry with backoff on rate limits and server errors, and
the Robinhood bridge has timeouts. The app also counts each source's successes and failures;
after three failures in a row it pauses requests for 30 seconds instead of hammering a source
that is down. A failed quote is never quietly replaced: pricing and tickets get the error;
read-only views of positions may show the last quote received in this session (up to three
days old), marked STALE with the reason. A failed history refresh keeps using the saved bars
and says so (`bar_info.refresh_failed`). Nothing is made up for a stock with nothing saved.

Where it shows: position marks (`mark.freshness`, a tag beside the price), the positions list
(stale or undated prices are tagged), portfolio risk (`coverage.stale`, a STALE_PRICES warning;
the figures still count but are named), the Analyze screen's quote and data checks,
`GET /v2/data/{symbol}` (`quote_freshness`, `bars_freshness`) and `GET /v2/source/health`
(successes, failures, the last error and any pause; no credentials).

## Reconciliation, repair and data health (2.3)

Fills, with adjustments and option events applied as they are replayed, are the accounting
record. A position's status, open quantity, average prices, realized profit, fees and open
and close times are a cache of them.

`GET /v2/diagnostics/reconciliation` (`reconcile.diagnose`) only reads. It recomputes every
position from its fills and lists:

| Key | What it lists |
| --- | --- |
| `ledger_mismatches` | stored figures that differ from the fills, with both values |
| `lifecycle_issues` | positions opened again after closing (older versions allowed it); kept as recorded |
| `invalid_states` | positions whose fills no longer account (for example a sale larger than the holding) |
| `possible_duplicate_fills` | same position, side, size, price and time with no broker id; not merged, because two orders can fill alike |
| `journal_mismatches` | option-journal entries whose mirrored position has different fills |
| `unresolved_options` | bought options past expiration with contracts open |
| `market_data_gaps` | open positions with no saved price history, or whose refresh failed |
| `missing_snapshots`, `hindsight_snapshots`, `unavailable_snapshots` | as `GET /v2/snapshots/status` |
| `stale_snapshots` | effective snapshots that no longer apply, such as an exit on a position open again (2.3.1) |
| `rule_breaks` | rows the 2.3 database rules would refuse |
| `database` | `PRAGMA integrity_check` and `PRAGMA foreign_key_check` |

with `counts`, a `summary` of each list's length, `needs_repair` (ledger or journal
mismatches) and `needs_attention`.

`POST /v2/diagnostics/repair` with `{"confirm": true}` (`reconcile.repair`) copies the
database first and does nothing if the copy fails; then re-reads mismatched journal entries
into their positions and recomputes each mismatched position from its fills. It never
changes, merges or deletes a fill you recorded, and leaves reopened positions as they are. A
position mirrored from the option journal has its fills re-read from the journal, its record;
each fill changed that way is kept as a JOURNAL_SYNC correction. Each item is repaired in its
own transaction, so one that cannot be (its fills do not account) is listed in `failed` and the
rest go ahead. What it changed and the copy's path go in `maintenance_log`. A second run
changes nothing.

**A position whose fills no longer account** (data written by an older version, such as a
fill below the smallest recordable quantity) does not stop anything else (2.3.1). The
positions list returns it under `invalid` with its fills, and the Positions screen shows it
with a Correct button for each fill; portfolio risk, the daily stop, the ticket's open risk,
the reconciliation report, snapshot status and the data health view count it as not measured.
Correcting the wrong fill brings it back.

`GET /v2/health/data` gathers the data source's health, the freshness of each open
position's price, positions that could not be valued and options whose chain lacked the
contract, saved daily history for held and watched stocks, snapshot status, the schema
version and latest migration with the copy taken before it, the reconciliation summary,
recent repairs and recent warnings from the log. It holds no token, password or setting
value.

## Logging (2.3, `events.py`)

Operational events are logged one per line as `event=NAME key=value ...` under the
`tradeassist.events` logger:

| Event | Level | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `provider_failure` | WARNING | a data-source request failed |
| `stale_market_data` | INFO | a stale or undated price was shown, labelled |
| `missing_valuation` | INFO | a position could not be priced |
| `risk_calculation_failed` | WARNING | a risk figure could not be worked out |
| `snapshot_failed` | WARNING | lifecycle snapshots could not be brought up to date |
| `migration_applied` / `migration_failed` | INFO / ERROR | a schema migration ran (with its backup) or was rolled back |
| `invalid_transition` | INFO | a change to a position was refused (expected; no stack trace) |
| `correction_recorded` | INFO | a fill was corrected, with the reason |
| `repair_applied` | WARNING | stored figures were repaired from the fills |
| `invalid_position` | WARNING | a position's fills no longer account; it is listed apart (2.3.1) |
| `position_reopened` | INFO | a confirmed split or adjustment opened a closed position again (2.3.1) |
| `integrity_refused`, `database_error` | WARNING | a database rule refused a write; the database failed |
| `cross_site_refused` | WARNING | a request from another web page was refused (2.3.1) |

Values that look like credentials are replaced with `[redacted]`: an Authorization header's
value with any scheme, `Bearer` and `Basic` values, `key=value`, `key: value` and JSON
`"key": "value"` for token, access and refresh tokens, API keys, client secrets, passwords
(`password`, `passwd`, `pwd`), cookies and session ids; `code`, `key`, `sig` and `state` in
query strings; JSON web tokens; and long mixed letter-and-digit strings. `run.py` applies the
filter to every log line from any module and to uvicorn's own loggers, and it redacts
tracebacks as well as messages. Since 2.3.1 a data source's error is redacted before anything
else sees it (the health views, error replies, position marks), not only in the log. Events carry ids and symbols, not account details.
Warnings and errors are also kept in memory (the last 100) for the Data health screen.

Database errors reach the screen as actionable messages: a rule or uniqueness clash is a 409
("Nothing was saved"), a busy or locked database a 503 ("try again in a moment").

## Requests from other web pages (2.3.1)

The app listens on 127.0.0.1 only, but a web page open in the same browser could still try to
reach it. Two checks stop that: requests must name this computer as their host (127.0.0.1,
localhost or ::1), which defeats a page that points its own host name at 127.0.0.1 (DNS
rebinding); and a request that changes anything (any method but GET, HEAD and OPTIONS) is
refused with a 403 when the browser says it came from another page (an `Origin` that is not
this app, or `Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site`). The app's own page is unaffected.

2.4 adds a third: reads under `/api/` and `/v2/` that the browser marks as made by another
site's page (`Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site` or `same-site`) are refused too, because scans, the
market make-up and news cost requests to the data source and to Finnhub. Typing an address or
calling the API from a script sends no such header and is unaffected. The test client's host
name (`testserver`) is accepted only inside the test suite.

## Trend Engine V2 (Sprint 3)

`trend.py` gives each day one of seven states (STRONG_UP, UP, UP_WEAKENING, NEUTRAL,
DOWN_DEVELOPING, DOWN, STRONG_DOWN), the evidence behind it and how settled it is. Every
input uses only that day's bar and earlier ones; a test checks that the states for the first
k days are the same whether or not later days exist.

Evidence, with the points each kind can give to the bullish or the bearish side:

| Kind | Points | Readings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swing structure | 22 | latest swing high and low labels (HH/HL, LH/LL), and breaks of the last higher low or lower high |
| Moving averages | 22 | close against the 20-day (4), 50-day (5) and 200-day (6) averages; 50-day against 200-day (4); slope of the 50-day (3) |
| Momentum | 20 | MACD against its signal (5); histogram direction (3); MACD against zero (4); RSI 14 (4); 20-day change (4) |
| Relative strength | 16 | return less SPY's over three months (6) and one month (4); less the sector fund's over three months (6) |
| Volume | 12 | volume on up days against down days over 20 days (7); on-balance volume over 20 days (5) |
| Validated patterns | 8 | patterns that fired in the last five days and passed the pattern lab |

Graded readings (RSI, slopes, relative strength, volume) earn points in proportion up to a
full-scale value. The bullish and bearish scores are the points each side won as a share of
the points that could be scored that day (0 to 100 each); readings without data (no sector
fund, no lab run, too little history) are left out and lower `coverage`. **Scores are
evidence points, not probabilities.** RSI above 70 is not counted as bearish: in a trend,
overbought is more often strength than a top.

State machine on the net score (bullish less bearish): a state is entered at 25 and kept
down to 15; strong states need 55, the swing structure and the order of price, 50-day and
200-day to agree. An uptrend never jumps to DOWN: it goes through UP_WEAKENING (net below 15
or the uptrend structure broken) or DOWN_DEVELOPING (net at −25 or below). DOWN_DEVELOPING
becomes DOWN once the swing structure turns down or after five days. A downtrend passes
through NEUTRAL before UP. Below half coverage the state is NEUTRAL.

Confirmation level: EARLY (under three days in the state), DEVELOPING (under ten),
ESTABLISHED. Confidence (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW) rises with coverage, a clear net score, ADX of
25 or more and time in the state, and falls with balanced evidence, ADX under 18, daily
ranges in the top 15% of the past year, and an unfinished bar; the reasons are listed.

When today's bar is unfinished the response has both `provisional` (with today's bar so
far) and `confirmed` (the last finished bar), and `changed_today` says whether they differ.
Changes of state are listed for finished bars only.

Relative strength uses SPY, QQQ (shown, not scored) and the stock's Select Sector SPDR fund
from a fixed list of about 270 large companies (`sectors.py`), or from the SEC industry code
when SEC lookups are on.

API: `GET /v2/trend/{symbol}` and `GET /v2/analysis/{symbol}` (trend, data checks, quote,
and the sections later sprints add). Position snapshots now record the trend state,
bullish and bearish scores and relative strength. Screen: **Analyze a stock**.

## Market regime (Sprint 4)

`regime.py` names what the market is doing each day, from SPY's trend state (the trend
engine run on SPY), QQQ's as a cross-check, and volatility:

| Regime | When |
| --- | --- |
| BULL_CALM | SPY uptrend or strong uptrend; volatility calm or normal |
| BULL_VOLATILE | SPY uptrend; volatility elevated or stressed |
| RANGE | SPY without a trend |
| TRANSITION | SPY uptrend weakening or downtrend developing, or SPY and QQQ in opposite trends |
| BEAR_CALM | SPY downtrend; volatility calm or normal |
| BEAR_VOLATILE | SPY downtrend; volatility elevated or stressed |

Volatility: the VIX close when the source has VIX history (under 15 calm, 15 to 20 normal,
20 to 30 elevated, above 30 stressed); otherwise SPY's 20-day realized volatility ranked
against the past year (under 30% of days calm, under 70% normal, under 90% elevated,
otherwise stressed; 35% annualized is always stressed). History that does not look like VIX
is ignored. The demo source has no VIX. Breadth is not included. The regime is causal, like
the trend states, and has a provisional reading when SPY's bar is unfinished.

Indicators in context (`regime.interpret`): RSI, the 52-week range position and the
Bollinger bands are read for the stock's own trend state and the regime. Overbought in an
uptrend reads as strength, in a range as stretched, in a downtrend as a rally that tends to
fade; near the 52-week high while the market falls reads as relative strength; above the
upper band in an uptrend reads as walking the band; squeezed bands say a bigger move often
follows, not which way. These readings explain; they add nothing to the trend scores.

API: `GET /v2/regime`; `/v2/analysis/{symbol}` adds `regime` and `interpretation`. Position
snapshots record `market_regime`.

## Signal history and calibration (Sprint 5)

`signalbook.py` keeps every past signal in `signal_occurrences` (migration 3): each pattern
completing and each change of trend state, on finished bars only, with the trend state, net
score and market regime that day and what followed (returns 5, 10 and 20 trading days on,
SPY over the same days, and the best and worst prices in the next 20 days). Outcomes are
measured as in the pattern lab: entry at the next day's open, exit at the close h days
after the signal. Rebuilding replaces a stock's rows.

`POST /v2/signals/history/build` (`{"symbols": [...], "years": 5}`, default the
watchlist) builds it; the Pattern lab screen has a button. The trend states used for it
leave out the validated-pattern points, because the lab found those verdicts on the same
history.

`GET /v2/signals/{symbol}` lists what fired on the stock in the last ten bars (patterns and
changes of trend state, each CONFIRMED or PROVISIONAL; trend changes are found without
validated-pattern points, as in the history) and, for each, how its past cases
turned out over 10 days: across all stocks, in the current market regime, and on this stock
alone, as "went the signal's way in X% of N cases" with a 95% Wilson interval, average
return, return against SPY and best and worst moves. Fewer than 30 cases are flagged.
Overlapping cases are not independent; the number of separate periods is given.

Calibration: every day's net score (without pattern points) is put in a range (−100 to −50,
−50 to −25, −25 to 0, 0 to 25, 25 to 50, 50 to 100) and the share of days followed by a
gain over 10 and 20 days is counted, also by regime. The score is called **calibrated**
only when the ranges with at least 30 days rise in order (within two points) and the
highest range beat the lowest significantly (p < 0.05, one-sided Welch test on
non-overlapping blocks of h trading days across all stocks, each block counted once, because
overlapping windows and stocks moving together on the same dates are not independent; on
correlated random walks it called noise calibrated in 0 of 60 trials). Otherwise it is
reported as not calibrated and must be read as a count of evidence only.
`GET /v2/signals/calibration` returns the table; `GET /v2/patterns/{symbol}` gives each
pattern's events on the stock, its record there and across all stocks, and the lab verdict.

Evidence score and historical frequency are always shown separately. Neither is a forecast.

**Dependence (2.3).** Past cases that overlap in time, or come from several stocks on the same
dates, are not independent, so N of them carry less information than N separate trials. The
frequency uses every case, but its 95% range (`frequency_low`, `frequency_high`) is a Wilson
interval on the effective number of separate periods (non-overlapping blocks of the horizon)
instead of on N: wider, and honest about the dependence. Since 2.3.1 the periods are weighted
by how many cases each holds, (sum of sizes)^2 / (sum of squared sizes): equal periods count
as their number, and one period holding most of the cases counts as little more than one. The interval on N alone is kept as
`naive_low`/`naive_high` for reference. The score ranges in the calibration table use the same
method.

**Held-out check (2.3; corrected in 2.3.1).** The check (in order, significant on time blocks)
is made on the earlier 70% of dates only, which also choose the lowest and highest usable
score ranges; the latest 30% are held out and used only to see whether those ranges point the
same way. The last horizon before the cut is left out of the check, because those cases'
outcomes are measured on held-out prices. (In 2.3.0 the check used every date, so the
held-out dates were part of what they were meant to confirm.) `calibration_status` is CALIBRATED only when the earlier dates pass
and the held-out dates agree; CALIBRATED_IN_SAMPLE_ONLY when they do not; NOT_CALIBRATED or
INSUFFICIENT_DATA otherwise. `checked_before` is the first held-out date. `calibrated` is true only for CALIBRATED, so the trade grade
counts past cases only then. Histories built before 2.3 keep their recorded yes or no until
rebuilt.

## Position evaluator and trade grades (Sprint 6)

`POST /v2/position/evaluate` grades a stock trade, long or short:

```json
{"symbol": "NVDA", "direction": "SHORT", "entry_price": 694.5, "invalidation_price": 738,
 "target_1": 580, "target_2": 556, "horizon_days": 20}
```

Every field but the symbol is optional: the entry defaults to the current price; the
invalidation to a quarter of a daily range (ATR) beyond the last swing low (long) or high
(short), or two ranges away when no swing is within six; targets to the first two support
or resistance levels in the way at least half the risk away, else two and three times the
risk. With `position_id` an existing stock position is graded with its own plan and average
cost.

| Component | Weight | Scored from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trend alignment | 30 | the trend state for this direction; less for a provisional or low-confidence reading |
| Market regime | 15 | how the regime favours longs or shorts |
| Reward to risk | 20 | target 1 against the invalidation: 1:1 scores 40, 2:1 80, 3:1 100 |
| Past cases | 15 | the score range's frequency, only if the score is calibrated; recent signals' records each way |
| Volatility and fit | 10 | stop distance in ATRs (under 0.5 or over 4 is penalised), unusual volatility, dollar volume |
| Catalysts | 10 | earnings within the holding period, within two trading days, or unknown |

Grade: A 80+, B 65+, C 50+, D 35+, else AVOID. Caps: AVOID for an invalidation or target on
the wrong side, target 1 under 0.75 times the risk, or data that failed its checks; D for a
short against a strong uptrend or a long against a strong downtrend; C for earnings within
two trading days. The response gives the levels (and whether they were yours or
suggested), reward to risk, a size that keeps the loss at the invalidation within
`MAX_RISK_PER_TRADE_PCT` of `ACCOUNT_SIZE`, each component with its reasons, analogs (past
changes into the same trend state), and what would improve the grade.

Shorts also get `short_sale_risk`: borrow availability, fees and short interest cannot be
seen by this app and are flagged as unknown; a run-up of more than 30% in a month (worse on
heavy volume), opening gaps of more than 1.5 ATRs in the past year, an uptrend against the
short and earnings within a month are flagged from the data.

The grade summarises the checks. It is not a probability.

## Options contract evaluator (Sprint 7)

`tradeassist/options/evaluator.py` finds the calls (bullish) or puts (bearish) that fit a
thesis about the stock. `POST /v2/options/evaluate`:

```json
{"symbol": "NVDA", "direction": "BULLISH", "target_price": 780, "invalidation_price": 650,
 "horizon_days": 20, "contracts": 1, "budget": 1000}
```

Target and invalidation default to the stock evaluator's suggestions. Expirations: the first
at least a week after the horizon date and those nearest two, three and four and a half times
the horizon (up to four), or the ones given in `expirations`. Candidates: delta between 0.10
and 0.90, nearest the stock price, up to 24 per expiration.

Each contract is valued with the simulator's Black-76 model in a scenario matrix: the stock
at the invalidation price, unchanged, at target 1 and at target 2; on the horizon date and
halfway there; implied volatility −5, 0 and +5 points. Entry at the midpoint; the spread
cost is shown apart. "If invalidated" is the worse of the two dates at the invalidation
price, never more than the premium. Also: return on premium at target, reward to risk,
breakevens at the horizon and at expiration, and the market-implied chance of profit at
expiration.

Rankings: **BEST THESIS MATCH** (largest return on premium at target 1 by the horizon),
**LOWEST COST** (cheapest that still makes money there), **BEST RISK-ADJUSTED** (most gained
at target per dollar lost if invalidated), **MOST CONSERVATIVE** (smallest share of the
premium lost if invalidated, with at least twice the horizon to expiration).

Avoid list, with reasons: no two-sided quote, spread over 10% of the midpoint, open interest
under 100, expiring before the horizon, losing money even at the target, over budget. Flags:
earnings before expiration (volatility usually falls after the report), paying at the target
only if volatility holds, needing the stock past the target to break even.

The exit rule is on the stock: exit when it closes through the invalidation price, whatever
the option is quoted at. The response also has the stock trade's own grade and the
market-implied chances of the stock being past the target and the invalidation price on the
horizon date (option-implied, not forecasts).

`POST /v2/options/compare` takes the same thesis plus `contracts_to_compare`
(`[{"option_type": "CALL", "strike": 760, "expiration": "2026-11-13"}]`, up to eight) and
values just those. Only bought calls and puts are evaluated. On the Analyze screen each pick
can be opened in the simulator or saved as a planned position with the thesis's levels.

## Portfolio risk (Sprint 8)

`GET /v2/portfolio/risk` adds up every open position (option-journal trades included):

- **Exposure** in stock dollars: shares times price (negative short); delta × 100 ×
  contracts × stock price for options. Gross, net, long and short totals, by stock and by
  sector (the sector funds of `sectors.py`).
- **SPY-equivalent dollars**: each exposure times its beta to SPY from the last six months
  of daily returns on finished bars.
- **Greeks**: theta per day, vega per volatility point, and the change in dollar delta for
  a 1% move (gamma), for the options.
- **Risk to invalidation**: the loss if each stock went to its invalidation price now
  (option repriced with the model there, never more than its value); unknown for stock
  without an invalidation price, the whole value for an option without one. Compared with
  `MAX_OPEN_RISK_PCT` of `ACCOUNT_SIZE`.
- **A bad day**: 1.645 standard deviations of daily P&L from the six-month covariance of
  returns and the exposures (deltas only; assumes the past six months are typical). Since 2.2
  also the historical figures; see "Risk models" below.
- **Correlation** of the holdings' daily returns; pairs above 0.8 held the same way are
  flagged as closer to one bet than two.
- **Stress tests**: market −5%/−10%/−20% with volatility +5/+10/+20 points, market
  +5%/+10% with volatility −2/−4, volatility +10 with the market flat, and the largest
  position gapping 20% against you. Stocks move their beta times the market; options are
  repriced with the simulator's model, using the chain's forward carry, rate and smile
  volatility, and measured as model(scenario) − model(now), so an unchanged market shows no
  profit or loss. `POST /v2/portfolio/stress` runs your own
  (`{"scenarios": [{"name": "...", "market_move_pct": -15, "iv_shift_points": 12,
  "moves": {"NVDA": -25}}]}`).
- **Warnings**: over the open-risk limit, one stock over 25% of the account, one sector over
  40% of gross exposure, positions through their invalidation price, options within seven
  days of expiration, time decay over 0.2% of the account a day, missing prices.
- **Gross and net** (2.2). Exposure is reported both ways, in total and by stock and sector
  (`by_symbol`, `by_sector`: `net`, `gross`, `long`, `short`, `positions`; `exposure` is the
  net, as before). Gross adds each position's delta dollars without their signs, so a long and
  an offsetting short of the same stock add up instead of hiding each other; net is the
  directional bet left. The one-stock (25% of the account) and one-sector (40% of gross)
  warnings use gross and mention the net when longs and shorts offset. Delta dollars, market
  value, notional (contracts x multiplier x stock price), option premium at risk and the
  liquidation estimate are separate measures, each defined in `definitions`, and are never
  added to one another.
- **Open risk by basis** (2.2). Each position's `open_risk` has a `risk_basis`: INVALIDATION
  (the loss if the stock went to the invalidation price now), PREMIUM (a bought option without
  an invalidation price, or already past it: its whole value, the most it can lose),
  CONFIGURED (stock without an invalidation price but with dollars at risk entered: your
  assumption, labelled as such), BEYOND_INVALIDATION (stock already through its invalidation
  price: the plan no longer bounds the loss, so it is unknown) or UNKNOWN. `risk_coverage`
  gives the total by basis, the positions not measured with the reason, and a conclusion that
  says whether it includes every position: over the limit on what is known is a breach;
  under it with anything unknown is "not known whether the whole is inside the limit".
- **Marks** (2.2). Each position's mark has the analytical price used for unrealized profit
  (`mark_basis`: bid/ask midpoint, last trade or close for stock; midpoint or model value for
  an option), the last trade, bid, ask, midpoint, spread, and `liquidation_price`: the bid for
  something held, the ask for a short to buy back (zero for an option with no bid). The
  position's `pnl` adds `liquidation_value` and `liquidation_unrealized`, and the portfolio
  `estimated_liquidation_value`. These are estimates from the quote, not fills.
- **Risk models** (2.2). Returns are taken only between consecutive closes: a day missing
  for one stock is left out of its pairs rather than becoming a zero return (2.1 carried the
  last close forward). Beta and correlation use the days both stocks have, and need 40;
  otherwise there is no beta (the position counts one for one, flagged) and the correlation
  is shown as n/a. `return_quality` flags short histories, missing days and runs of unchanged
  closes that suggest a stale feed. `bad_day` gives, on the days every holding has a return,
  the normal-approximation figure (1.645 standard deviations), the historical 5th percentile
  loss, the expected shortfall beyond it and the worst day; all are estimates, not maximum
  losses. A holding with no usable price history is named in `bad_day.left_out` with its
  exposure, `complete` is false and a BAD_DAY_PARTIAL warning says the figures understate a
  bad day (2.3.1). Stress tests never take a stock below zero (`clamped`), and a bought option
  never loses more than its value, also when it has no volatility to reprice with (2.3.1).
- **Positions without a price** (2.1.1). Every position comes back with the same fields.
  A figure that cannot be worked out (no quote, no delta from the chain) is `null`, never 0,
  with `priced`, `missing` and `missing_fields` saying what is unknown and why. Totals add
  up the positions that have the figure, and a total no position could be measured for is
  `null`, not 0 (2.3.1); `coverage` (COMPLETE, PARTIAL or UNAVAILABLE) lists
  the positions left out, `totals.unknown_counts` says how many each total misses, and a
  warning such as "Portfolio risk is incomplete because 1 position does not have a current
  price" leads the page. Open risk says whether it covers every position
  (`open_risk_complete`): a known total over the limit is a breach whatever else is unknown,
  and a known total under it with anything unknown is not reported as inside the limit.
  Stress tests reprice what they can and list the rest in `excluded`. Options are scaled by
  their own contract multiplier.

**Daily loss** (`DAILY_LOSS_MODE`): `REALIZED_PLUS_UNREALIZED` (default) counts what was
closed today plus today's change in open positions; `REALIZED_ONLY` counts closed trades
only. Quantity held at midnight is measured from the previous close (stock: the last daily
close before today; option: the chain's previous close, or an estimate from today's price,
delta, theta and the stock's move), so closing today a position bought last week counts only
today's move. With portfolio risk on, the classic ticket's daily loss check uses this figure
too, so a large open loss stops new trades before it is realised. Screen: **Portfolio risk**.

The measurement and its completeness are reported separately (2.1.1):

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `realized_today` | Profit on fills closed today against their average cost. Always known: it needs only the fills. |
| `unrealized_change_today` | Today's change in what is open, for the positions it could be measured for; `unrealized_complete` says whether that is all of them. |
| `known_total` (also `counted`) | The day's result over the positions measured completely: realized plus unrealized change, or realized only in `REALIZED_ONLY` mode. |
| `loss_status` | `OK`, `WARN` (three quarters of `DAILY_LOSS_STOP_PCT`) or `STOP` (the stop), from `known_total`. A measured loss that reaches the stop is a STOP whatever else is unknown. |
| `coverage_status` | `COMPLETE`, `PARTIAL` or `UNAVAILABLE`: whether every position's day could be measured. |
| `status` | For older callers: `loss_status`, except that OK with anything unmeasured is `INCOMPLETE`, never a plain OK. |
| `missing` | Each position not measured, with the reason: no current price, a stale price, no previous close (including history that could not be refreshed, whose last close may be days old), or fills that no longer account. |
| `positions_evaluated_pct`, `capital_evaluated_pct` | Share of positions, and of capital at cost, that was measured. |

A position held overnight needs yesterday's close; without it its day is unknown and it is
listed, not counted as zero. Since 2.3.1 a saved copy of a price (shown because the source
failed) does not measure the day either; a quote the source gives now counts, however long
since it changed, and is labelled. Yesterday's close from saved history that could not be
refreshed is used only if that history reaches the weekday before today. If the positions
cannot be read at all, the result is UNAVAILABLE with only the journal's closed trades known,
never a complete OK. In `REALIZED_ONLY` mode nothing is ever missing, because realized
profit comes from the fills (2.1 counted nothing for such a position even in that mode).

`INCOMPLETE_RISK_POLICY` decides what the trade ticket does when today's loss cannot be fully
measured: `WARN` (default) lists it under `risk_notes`, sets `within_limits` to false and
never shows "inside all of your limits"; `BLOCK` also makes it a limit the ticket breaks
(`daily_loss_incomplete`), which you can override on purpose and which is then recorded in
the journal. The app places no orders, so the policy only changes what the ticket says.

**The ticket's open-risk limit** (2.3.1) adds to the journal's open premium and saved plans the
open risk of every other position on the Positions screen, each on its stated basis (to the
invalidation price, the premium, or the dollars you entered); journal mirrors are not counted
twice. Before 2.3.1 it counted the journal only, so a large stock position could leave the
check passing. A position whose risk cannot be measured, or that is priced from a saved copy,
is named in a note (`open_risk_incomplete`), handled by `INCOMPLETE_RISK_POLICY` like the daily
loss. The per-trade limit still counts only the journal's risk in the same stock: an option on
shares you hold is often a hedge, and counting the shares against it would block the trade
that reduces risk.

## Journal analytics (Sprint 9)

`GET /v2/journal/stats` covers every closed unified position (stock long and short, calls
and puts, and option-journal trades through their mirrors):

- Totals: trades, wins and losses, win rate, average win and loss, **profit factor** (gross
  wins over gross losses; none when there are no losses), **expectancy** per trade in
  dollars and in R (trades without a risk are left out of R), total, largest win and loss,
  longest losing streak, average days held, equity curve of closed results and its deepest
  drawdown.
- **MAE and MFE**: the worst and best the stock moved against and for each trade while it
  was held, from daily highs and lows after the entry day to the exit day, in percent and in
  R (the distance from entry to the invalidation price). Calls and puts are measured on the
  stock, because option prices have no daily history here.
- Breakdowns: by setup, by bet on a rise or a fall, stock or options, trend state and
  market regime at entry (from the ENTRY snapshot), and holding time.
- Habits, each with results with and without it: held after the stock closed through the
  invalidation price, exited before target 1 without being invalidated, added at a worse
  price, risk above the per-trade limit, traded against the trend at entry, overrode a
  ticket limit (option journal).
- Under 20 closed trades every figure is flagged as unreliable.

Definitions (2.3, `analytics.DEFINITIONS`, also in the response): a **trade** is one closed
position, from its first fill to the fill that closed it, however many fills it took; its
result is the **net economic profit**: realized from the fills after their fees, plus borrow
fees, dividends and cash adjustments recorded on it (`trading_pnl` and `financing` are given
apart). Wins are above zero, losses below. R is the net result over the initial risk. MAE and
MFE use daily highs and lows from the day after entry to the exit day, so the entry day's own
range is not counted; since 2.3.1 the exit day's range is left out too when the exit came
during the session, because its high or low may have come after the exit (2.3.0 could credit
a trade with a target reached after it was sold). An open position's figures include today's
bar so far.

Habits are checked only where they can be: each is True, False, or not checked (no price
history, no entry-time context from a point-in-time snapshot, no risk, or it does not apply).
"With it" and "without it" compare trades checked and found with and without the habit;
trades that could not be checked are left out of both and counted as `not_checked`, never
treated as "did not happen". `coverage` gives how many trades have entry context,
excursions, an R multiple and known borrow costs, and the notes name shorts whose borrow
costs are not fully recorded.

`GET /v2/positions/{id}/analytics`: one position's MAE and MFE so far, its habits, and its
entry snapshot beside the latest exit or review snapshot.

Fills recorded after the fact get their ENTRY and EXIT snapshots rebuilt from the price
history. Up to 2.1 that was **as of the fill date's close**, which for a fill during the day
uses data from after it; since 2.1.1 it is as of the last daily bar that had finished before
the fill (see "Lifecycle snapshots" above), and older snapshots are marked as hindsight.
Option quotes and Greeks have no history here and are left out of such snapshots.

The Journal screen shows these analytics below the option journal; the Positions screen
shows each position's own.

## Backtest V2 (Sprint 10)

`backtest_v2.py` runs strategies written as conditions, on the classic engine
(`backtest.py`: next-open entries, stops before targets, gap fills, slippage, cooldown).

`POST /v2/backtest`:

```json
{"symbols": ["AAPL", "MSFT"], "years": 3, "monte_carlo": true,
 "strategy": {"long": [{"field": "trend_state", "op": "in", "value": ["UP", "STRONG_UP"]},
                       {"field": "rsi", "op": "<", "value": 40}],
              "short": [{"field": "close", "op": "crosses_below", "value": "sma50"}],
              "regimes": ["BULL_CALM", "BULL_VOLATILE"]},
 "params": {"stop_loss_pct": 0.06, "take_profit_pct": 0.15, "holding_period_days": 60,
            "short_borrow_rate_pct": 0.5}}
```

or `"preset"`: `trend_following`, `pullback`, `breakdown_short`, `both_ways`.
`GET /v2/backtest/fields` lists fields, operators, presets and patterns. Conditions in a
list must all hold (AND); fields are the indicator columns, the 5- and 20-day change, the
trend engine's state and scores (without validated-pattern points), the market regime and
patterns (`{"field": "pattern", "op": "fired", "value": "hammer"}`). Operators: `>`, `>=`,
`<`, `<=`, `crosses_above`, `crosses_below` (against a number or another field), `in`,
`not_in` (for states), `fired`. Nothing is evaluated as code. Long and short on the same day
cancel. `regimes` keeps only entries on days in those regimes. All reads use finished bars.

Shorts pay `short_borrow_rate_pct` a year (0.5 by default here, 0 in the classic backtest)
on the value borrowed, by the calendar day, recorded on each trade as `borrow_cost`.

Monte Carlo: 1,000 block-bootstrap paths of the daily returns (20-day blocks), giving the
5th, 50th and 95th percentiles of return and deepest drawdown, the chance of a loss and of a
drawdown over 20%. It shows how much the result depends on the order of the days, not
whether the edge is real.

`POST /v2/backtest/walkforward` adds `grid` (default stop 4/6/8% by target 10/15/20%),
`train_days`, `test_days` and `anchored` (training from the start instead of a rolling
window). Only the test windows count.

**Reproducible runs (2.3, `runs.py`).** Each backtest, walk-forward, pattern-lab run and
signal-history build is recorded in `research_runs` with three hashes: the settings
(strategy, parameters, symbols, dates, costs and execution assumptions, Monte Carlo seed, data
source and the app and engine versions), the price history it read (per symbol: first and
last date, number of bars and a hash of every bar) and its deterministic output. The response
carries them in `run`. The same settings and data always give the same result hash; a test
checks it. `GET /v2/research/runs`, `GET /v2/research/runs/{id}` and
`GET /v2/research/runs/compare?a=&b=` list runs and say why two results differ: the settings,
the price history (and for which symbols), or neither, which would be a bug. The Monte Carlo
seed is fixed at 7.

Option strategies are not backtested. `options/history.py` defines
`HistoricalOptionDataProvider`; the only provider says no connected source has past option
prices. With `FEATURE_OPTIONS_BACKTEST` off an option backtest answers 404, with it on but
no provider 400 with that reason. They are never approximated from today's volatility.

## Trend reversals: potential bottoms and tops (2.1)

A true bottom or top is only certain afterwards. `reversal.py` reads the evidence that tends
to come with one, on both sides at once (a bottom ends a fall, a top ends a rise), bar by bar
and causally: the reading on any past day uses only that day's bar and earlier ones, so the
engine's past calls can be counted.

### Evidence (100 points per side)

| Kind | Evidence | Points | Seen when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exhaustion | A long prior move | 10 | The move to the extreme of the last 40 bars, from the opposite extreme in the 60 before, is 4 or more daily ranges (ATR), points rising to full at 6, and no more than 60% of it has been retraced |
| | RSI at an extreme | 8 | RSI 30 or below (70 or above for a top) in the last five days |
| | Stretched | 7 | A close 2 ATR or more from the 20-day average, or outside the Bollinger band, in the last five days |
| | RSI divergence | 10 | The leg's low is below the last swing low but RSI is 3 or more points higher there, and under 50 |
| | MACD divergence | 5 | The MACD histogram is less negative at the new low than at the last one |
| | Climax day | 10 | In the last five days: twice the usual volume, a range of 1.5 ATR or more, a 20-day low, and a close 40% or more off the low |
| | Reversal candle | 10 | Hammer, bullish engulfing, Bollinger reclaim or RSI leaving oversold in the last three days (the mirror set for a top) |
| Confirmation | First swing the other way | 10 | A higher low (lower high for a top) confirmed in the last 30 days |
| | Swing structure broken | 15 | A close above the last lower high (below the last higher low) in the last 20 days; 10 points instead if the trend engine has left its downtrend (uptrend) |
| | Back across the 20-day average | 5 | The close is above a rising 20-day EMA |
| | Back across the 50-day average | 5 | The close is above the 50-day average |
| | Volume favours the turn | 5 | Up-day volume is 1.2 times down-day volume or more over ten days |

### Stages

| Stage | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| NONE | No long enough move, or most of it already retraced |
| WATCH | A long move that is stretched or at an RSI extreme, with no reversal sign yet |
| CANDIDATE ("potential bottom" or "top") | The move has paused (the extreme is not today's bar and the close is half an ATR or more off it) and there are two of divergence, climax and reversal candle, or one of them with RSI at an extreme or a stretch. Live for ten bars |
| CONFIRMED | The swing structure broke, or a swing turn or the trend engine's turn came with the close back across a rising 20-day EMA. Reported for fifteen bars |
| FAILED | A close beyond the candidate's extreme, or beyond what a confirmation rests on. No new candidate for three bars unless a climax comes |

Today's unfinished bar never produces a confirmed signal: the stage used for trading and the
history is the one on the last finished bar, and a different reading from today's bar so far
is shown beside it as provisional.

### Levels for a trade

The invalidation is a quarter of an ATR beyond the reversal's extreme, or beyond the latest
higher low (lower high) that has formed since the extreme and is at least one ATR from the
price, which is nearer and more useful once the turn is under way. Target 1 is the first of
resistance (support), the last lower high (higher low) and the 50- and 200-day averages that
is at least half the risk away (two times the risk if there is none); target 2 is the next
such level at least half the risk beyond target 1 (else one more time the risk).

### History

New candidates and confirmations are recorded in the signal history as
`reversal:bottom_candidate`, `reversal:bottom_confirmed`, `reversal:top_candidate` and
`reversal:top_confirmed` (signal engine `signals-2.1`; rebuild the history to add them). The
Analyze screen shows, for each, how often the stock moved the expected way over 5, 10 and 20
days on this stock over five years and across the stocks in the history, with Wilson 95%
ranges. Migration 4 adds `atr_pct`, the daily range as a share of the price on the day, to
each recorded occurrence, and the lowest and highest close over each horizon.

### Scoring a call or put (`options/reversal_score.py`)

Calls for a potential bottom, puts for a potential top. The options evaluator values each
contract with the reversal's levels (Sprint 7's scenario matrix), and each is scored:

| Check | Points | Full points when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reversal setup | 30 | Confirmed (stage weight 1.0; candidate 0.65, watch 0.3, none 0) and 60 or more evidence points; 60% stage, 40% evidence |
| Payoff | 20 | Reward to risk 3 or more at target 1 (70%) and 200% or more on the premium (30%) |
| Target in reach | 15 | Target 1 within half the move option prices expect by the horizon (at-the-money implied volatility times the square root of time); none at two expected moves |
| Breakeven | 10 | Breaks even by the horizon with no move; none if it needs 120% of the move to target 1 |
| Price of volatility | 5 | Implied volatility at or below 0.9 times the 20-day actual; scaled down by a high IV rank when 30 or more days are recorded |
| Time decay | 10 | Loses nothing by the horizon if the stock does not move; none at half the premium |
| Liquidity | 10 | Spread at most a few percent of the midpoint (70%) and open interest of 1,000 or more (30%) |

Verdicts: GOOD 70 or more, FAIR 50 to 69, POOR under 50. Earnings before expiration take
10 points off. AVOID whatever the score for anything on the evaluator's avoid list (no
two-sided quote, a wide spread, little open interest, expiring before the horizon, losing
money at the target, over budget). Caps, whatever the score:

- no potential bottom or top on the last finished bar: at best POOR; only stretched (WATCH): at best FAIR;
- target 1 more than 1.5 expected moves away: at best FAIR;
- losing more than half the premium if the stock does not move: at best FAIR;
- an invalidation more than 4 ATR away (the turn is already well under way): at best FAIR;
- with 30 or more past cases, fewer than 35% of them profitable for this contract: at best FAIR
  (with fewer cases the same finding is shown as a caution).

Contracts are ranked by verdict, then score. The score summarises these checks; it is not a
probability.

### Gain and loss, and the simulation

For the best contract (and any you name): the gain or loss if the stock reaches target 2 or
target 1 by the horizon, target 1 halfway there, target 1 with implied volatility five
points lower or higher, no move, and the exit at the invalidation price; the full simulator
view (profit and loss by price and date, Greeks, market-implied odds) to the horizon; and
past cases: the stock's move over the horizon (5, 10 or 20 days, the largest not over it;
none for a horizon under 5 days) after each past case of the same reading, rescaled by the
ratio of this stock's daily range to the case's, applied to today's price and valued with
today's implied volatility. A case whose lowest close (highest for a top) within those days
reached the invalidation counts as an exit there, as the exit rule is on the close. Past
cases come from the signal history when it has 30 or more, else from this stock (8 or more).
Gaps through the stop and changes in implied volatility are not modelled. Migration 4 stores
the lowest and highest close over 5, 10 and 20 days with each occurrence for this.

### API

| Route | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v2/reversal/{symbol}` | Both sides on the last finished bar (and today's provisional reading), evidence, levels, the last year's stages and the past cases |
| `POST /v2/reversal/options` | `{symbol, side: auto/bottom/top, horizon_days, contracts, budget, target_price, target_2, invalidation_price, expirations, earnings_date, contracts_to_score: [{option_type, strike, expiration}]}`: scored contracts, the best one with its outcomes, past cases and simulation |
| `GET /v2/reversal/scan?symbols=A,B` | The reading for each stock on the watchlist (or those given), strongest first |

## Options lab (2.4)

The **Options lab** screen (`options/volatility.py`, `options/lab.py`, `static/lab.js`) shows
what a stock's option prices imply, from the chains the app already reads. Every figure is
what the market's prices imply under a lognormal model (Black-76 on the forward), not a
forecast.

For up to eight expirations, chosen to cover the nearest two and then about a week, two
weeks, a month, 45 and 60 days, three months, six months and a year:

| Figure | How it is worked out |
| --- | --- |
| At-the-money volatility | The average implied volatility of the call and put at the listed strike nearest the forward, two-sided quotes only |
| Expected move | One standard deviation of the price at expiration: spot x volatility x sqrt(years). About two times in three the stock ends inside the range, if the prices are right |
| Straddle | The at-the-money call plus put at their midpoints: what the market charges for a move either way, about 0.8 of the standard deviation |
| 25-delta skew | Implied volatility at a put delta of -0.25 minus a call delta of 0.25, each interpolated between the two listed contracts either side. Positive (puts dearer) is the usual tilt for stocks |
| Term structure | The front expiration (at least a week out) against the one nearest 75 days: upward, inverted (an event priced soon, or stress) or flat (within one volatility point) |
| 30-day volatility | At-the-money volatility interpolated in total variance between the expirations either side of 30 days |
| Realized volatility | Annualised standard deviation of daily log returns over 10, 20 and 60 trading days, from finished bars. Implied minus realized is shown against the 20-day figure; a stock that has not moved shows no ratio |
| Implied earnings move | The extra variance in the first expiration after the report: e^2 = s1^2 t1 - s0^2 t1, with s0 from an expiration before the report, or, when every expiration includes it, the base from the first two after it, (s2^2 t2 - s1^2 t1) / (t2 - t1). An expiration on the report day is left out, since it depends on whether the report is before the open or after the close. Not measured, with the reason, when the term structure carries no extra variance |
| IV rank | As before: from the daily snapshots' recorded volatility, shown only once enough days are recorded |

**Monte Carlo check of one contract.** Simulates risk-neutral stock paths (drift from the
chain's forward, the contract's own volatility), with antithetic pairs and a fixed seed (7),
so the same inputs always give the same result. Paths are 2,000 to 100,000 (20,000 by
default), checked once a trading day; a run is capped at five million path-steps, and the
checks are thinned (and the result says so) beyond that. It reports:

- the simulated value with its standard error, next to Black-76 from the same inputs, and
  whether they agree within three standard errors (a check of the model, not a better price);
- the chance of finishing in the money, simulated and by formula;
- the chance of touching the strike: on a closing price (simulated), with the
  Broadie-Glasserman-Kou formula for daily checks next to it, and at any moment (the
  reflection formula), which is always higher;
- where the stock ends (a histogram with the strike and today's price), and, at the
  midpoint or a price you give, the share of paths that end with a profit, the middle and
  the 5th to 95th percentile outcomes, and the model value minus the price.

"Add the implied earnings move" puts one lognormal jump of the implied size at the close of
the report day, timed on the option's own clock (to 4 pm New York on expiration), and takes its
variance out of the contract's volatility so it is not counted twice. The variance is removed
only when the jump is added; when it is not (the report has passed, is on or after the
expiration day, or no move can be measured), the result says why;
Black-76 is then compared at the combined volatility, and the touch formulas (which have no
jumps) are not shown. One volatility applies to every path: fat tails and changes in
volatility are not modelled.

Nothing on this screen places or records anything. `FEATURE_OPTIONS_LAB=0` switches it off.

| Route | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v2/options/{symbol}/volatility?expirations=8` | The term structure, skew, expected moves, straddles, implied earnings move, 30-day and realized volatility, IV rank, and any expiration that could not be read |
| `POST /v2/options/montecarlo` | `{symbol, expiration, option_type, strike, premium?, contracts, paths, seed, earnings_jump}`: the simulation above |

## Strategy builder (2.4)

A **Build a strategy** panel on the Simulate screen (`options/strategies.py`) turns a
strategy into real listed contracts and loads them into the simulator. Only contracts with
a two-sided quote are used; nothing is placed or recorded.

| Group | Strategies | How the strikes are chosen (defaults) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expect a rise | Long call; bull call spread (debit); bull put spread (credit) | Call at 0.50 delta; debit spread bought at 0.50 delta, sold about 2.5% of the price higher; credit spread sold at 0.30 delta with its wing about 2.5% lower |
| Expect a fall | Long put; bear put spread (debit); bear call spread (credit) | The mirror images |
| Expect a range | Iron condor; iron butterfly; butterflies with calls or puts; short straddle and strangle | Condor sold at 0.20 delta with wings about 2.5% beyond; iron butterfly and butterflies centred on the strike nearest the forward (or a strike you give) with even wings |
| Expect a big move | Long straddle; long strangle | At the money; strangle at 0.25 delta |
| Time decay | Calendars with calls or puts | Same strike nearest the forward; sold about 30 days out, bought about 30 days later |

The expiration is the listed one nearest the template's default (30 or 45 days) unless you
pick one; "for earnings" picks the first expiration after the next report. You can set the
delta, the wing width in dollars, the middle strike and how many. **Strikes down / up**
moves every strike one listed strike; **Wider / Narrower** moves the wings one listed strike;
a move that would cross strikes is refused with the reason.

Each result shows the legs with their bid, ask, midpoint and delta, the net debit or credit
at the midpoints and at the bid and ask, the most it can make and lose at expiration and the
breakevens (from the simulator: exact when every leg expires together, read from the model
at the first expiration for calendars), the market-implied chance of a profit at expiration,
and the **risk check**: the most it can lose against the per-trade limit (account size x
`MAX_RISK_PER_TRADE_PCT`), with how many would fit. Sold options without a matching option
bought are marked as unlimited loss and always fail the check. Strategies with sold options
can be simulated but not recorded on the Positions screen.

**Describe the trade.** A sentence such as "AAPL iron condor 45 days 16 delta 5 wide",
"sell a put spread on KO 30 dte", "AAPL 190/200 call spread 11/20" or "NVDA big move
after earnings" is read by rules: the ticker ($XYZ, a known ticker, or a capitalised
symbol; everyday words such as "low" are not taken as tickers), the strategy (named, or
from a view: a range means an iron condor, a big move a long straddle, a rise a long call;
the structure is read first and whether it is bought or sold from words anywhere in the
sentence, so "sell AAPL put spread" is a credit spread; a sold single option, a sold butterfly
or calendar, a reverse condor, or buy and sell together without a structure is refused with
the reason rather than built the other way round),
the expiration (days, weeks, months, a month name for its third Friday, a date, this or next
week, next month, after earnings), the delta, the width, how many and strikes written with
slashes (a short month/day pair next to strikes is read as a date). The screen shows what was
read and what was assumed. Strikes far from the stock price are refused, since "11/20" on
its own reads as strikes. When the rules find no strategy, an AI reader can be plugged in
(2.4 part 4); whatever it returns must be one of these templates and is built and checked
against the chain the same way.

`FEATURE_STRATEGY_BUILDER=0` switches the panel off.

| Route | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v2/strategies` | The templates, their legs and defaults |
| `POST /v2/strategies/build` | `{symbol, strategy, expiration?, dte?, far_expiration?, far_days?, contracts, delta?, width?, strikes?, center?, after_earnings?, adjust?: {op: up/down/wider/narrower/leg, leg?, by?}}`: the legs, prices, payoff, odds and risk check, and the request to send back with an `adjust` |
| `POST /v2/strategies/parse` | `{text, symbol?}`: what was read and assumed, and the built strategy |

## Today and Decide (2.5)

2.5 joins the screens into one decision path: Today (which way, which stock), Decide (the
thesis, shares or a contract, and what each might make or lose) and Price an option (the limit
to type at the broker). It adds no table, no migration and no key. Nothing in it places,
changes or cancels an order, reads balances or positions at a broker, paper-trades or invents
option prices.

### Today (`today.py`, `GET /v2/today/candidates?universe=watchlist|large_caps`)

The universe is the watchlist (`WATCHLIST` in `.env`) or the fixed list of 100 large
companies (`scanner.LARGE_CAPS`); other tickers are not accepted. For each stock the trend
engine is read on the last finished bar (`trend.confirmed`):

| Net score | Regime (last finished bar) | Opposite reversal | Lean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| unknown (`null`) | any | any | not measured (listed by name, never "no lean") |
| 0 | any | any | none: evidence balanced |
| > 0 | a bear regime (the two lowest rows of `REGIME_FIT_LONG`) | any | none: held back |
| > 0 | otherwise | a top at CANDIDATE or CONFIRMED | none: held back |
| > 0 | otherwise | otherwise | LONG |
| < 0 | the mirror image with `REGIME_FIT_SHORT` (a bull regime) and a bottom | | SHORT |

A reversal on the same side does not hold a lean back. The reversal is read only where it can
change the answer (it is the slow part). A stock whose reversal reading failed keeps its lean
and its row says "Reversal not checked". The long and short lists hold up to 8 stocks each,
largest |net score| first. Each row carries `past_10d` (2.5.1): the signal history's stored
10-day bucket for its net score (`signalbook.bucket_for`), with `low`, `high`, `cases`,
`frequency` (the share of cases higher 10 days later, not inverted for a short),
`frequency_low`, `frequency_high`, `enough` and `horizon`; `null` with no history, no bucket or
no cases. The buckets are read once per response; nothing is studied per stock. The screen
adds a "Higher 10 days later" column only when the history is built. The net score shown is
the trend engine's, which includes validated pattern-lab points when the lab has passed any;
the history was scored without them, so the two can differ slightly. The response also carries `long_total`, `short_total`, `no_lean`
(totals and each held-back stock with its reason), `not_measured`, `errors` (a stock with no
finished bar is listed here, not dropped and not counted), `history_built`, `regime`,
`universe_text` and `rule`. The screen also reads `/v2/regime` and `/v2/portfolio/risk` for
the regime and the daily stop and open risk lines.

### One thesis (`static/flow.js`, `static/v2.js` `thesisSection`)

`state.thesis` holds symbol, direction, entry, invalidation, two targets, holding days,
contracts and budget; blanks stay `null` ("suggest it"), never 0. Today and the Scanner call
`state.openDecide(symbol, direction)`. With a direction the thesis is set and graded at once;
turning a thesis round drops its prices (they belong to one side) and keeps the days,
contracts and budget. **Grade and find a contract** posts `FLOW.gradeRequest(thesis)` to
`/v2/position/evaluate`, then `FLOW.optionsRequest(thesis, grade.levels)` to
`/v2/options/evaluate`, so both read one set of levels; a typed entry also goes to the picker as
`entry_price` (2.5.1), used only for the chance the shares are ahead. Each run first clears the
last run's comparison and chart plan.

### Shares or a contract (`static/decide.js`)

Two rows on the thesis levels, valued on the horizon date:

- **Shares:** the grade's size for the per-trade risk.
- **Contract:** the best risk-adjusted pick, or the first pick when none is risk-adjusted.

| Column | Shares | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You pay | shares × entry (received, for a short) | premium at the midpoint |
| At target 1 / if invalidated | shares × the move from entry | `at_target`, `if_invalidated` |
| Option prices imply | `market.stock_profit_at_horizon` (from the entry), and `reach_target_1_by_horizon` | `market_implied_profit_at_horizon` |
| Outcome on the horizon date (2.5.1) | direction × shares × (each of `market.stock_percentiles_at_horizon` p10, p50, p90 − entry) | `implied_pnl_p10/p50/p90_at_horizon` |

The share trade has one price, the grade's entry: the typed one, or today's price when the box
was blank. The outcome column shows the middle outcome (50th percentile) and "nine in ten
between" the 10th and the 90th. A missing volatility or percentile shows "unknown", never $0.
It is never computed as P(target) × gain + P(invalidation) × loss.

**One forward for the stock (2.5.1, `options.evaluator.stock_outlook`).** The chances past
target 1 and past the invalidation, the expected price, the chance the shares are ahead, the
one-standard-deviation range and the three percentiles all come from one lognormal: centred on
the forward (spot grown at the carry the nearest analysed expiration implies) at its
at-the-money volatility. Before 2.5.1 the two chances used the stock price as the forward,
which on a hard-to-borrow name (forward well under the stock) gave two different chances for
one trade.

**The average.** Averaged over every price of that distribution (`simulate.implied_expectation`,
`market_odds(...)["expected_pnl"]`, the same weights), a contract is worth about what it costs.
It is shown once, in the footnote, not as a column.

- The stock is read at the at-the-money volatility of the nearest expiration analysed.
- Each contract is read at its own implied volatility, the one its price implies. Read at the
  at-the-money volatility, the skew alone would make out-of-the-money calls look cheap and
  puts dear.
- So the average comes out close to what is paid: carry to the horizon and the smile's fit to
  the midpoint, a percent or so. The screen says it is not an edge.
- An unknown volatility gives `null`, shown as "unknown", never $0.

**The three chances, kept apart and each named:**

- **Past cases:** shown only when the signal history is built (`history_built` on the grade;
  `null` when calibration is switched off). Otherwise the panel says so and offers to build
  it. The same-regime figure is used only with enough cases.
- **Option prices imply:** the columns above.
- **Show the path range:** the Monte Carlo of the contract, labelled as the same model, not a
  second opinion.

### The plan on the chart

After a grade, the trend chart on Decide runs on to the horizon date and adds:

- the targets and the invalidation;
- the one-standard-deviation range option prices imply by then (`market.range_1sd_at_horizon`),
  widening with the square root of time; the legend names the expiration its volatility comes
  from (`market.atm_expiration`, the soonest analysed expiration at least a week after the
  horizon);
- the compared contract's breakeven then.

The horizon is counted in weekdays from the last bar drawn. The trend ribbon and hover stay.

### Set the limit

Every contract card (picker, comparison, reversal option) maps the contract with
`FLOW.pricingRequest(symbol, contract, contracts, "buy")`, tested under Node. Its output is
the `/api/price` body. `state.openPricing(spec)` then fills Price an option (ticker, type,
expiration, strike, side, contracts) and prices it.

- An earnings date typed for another ticker is cleared.
- If the expiration or strike is no longer listed, the screen says so instead of pricing
  something else.
- Only the newest request draws the ladder.

## My trades (2.6)

Your Robinhood history, imported read-only and analysed on this computer:

- results by week, month, quarter or year over any date range;
- the strategies you used and the risk each carried;
- the kinds of trade that did better or worse than your average;
- every figure traceable to the statement rows behind it.

Nothing about the account is sent to the AI. Only ticker symbols leave the computer, to the
market-data source, for two prices: the stock's close on the day a trade was opened (where
the strike was), and the price now of shares still open.

### Sources (`tradehistory.py`, `robinhood_account.py`)

**Robinhood's account activity report** is the complete source. It is a CSV from Reports and
statements > Account activity report, generated in about 2 hours and at most 24.

- **Columns read:** Activity Date, Process Date, Settle Date, Instrument, Description, Trans
  Code, Quantity, Price, Amount.
- **Format details handled:** money in parentheses is negative, quantities may carry an "S"
  suffix, rows are listed newest first, and the file ends with a disclaimer.
- **Rows kept:**
  - shares: `Buy`, `Sell`;
  - options: `BTO`, `STC`, `STO`, `BTC`;
  - how options end: `OEXP` (expired), `OASGN` (assigned), `OEXCS`/`OEXER` (exercised);
  - `SPL`, a split; its quantity is the change in shares, negative for a reverse split.
- **Rows counted but not kept:** deposits, dividends, interest and fees.
- **Rows reported but not modelled:** mergers, spin-offs, symbol changes and shares moved
  between brokers (`MRGS`, `SOFF`, `SXCH`, `SPR`, `ACATI`, `ACATO`, …). Trades in those stocks
  may not add up, and the import says so.
- **Rows reported as unreadable:** an unreadable row is listed with its line and reason, and
  never guessed.

A row's key is its own content, so importing an overlapping report adds only new rows.
`broker_import_rows` records every import a row came in, so removing one import keeps the rows
another import also has.

A malformed CSV is refused with a clear message (HTTP 400, not 500). An amount with more than
15 digits makes its row unreadable. A file over 16 MB is refused before it is sent, and the
screen suggests importing a shorter period in parts. A file name is cut to 200 characters.

**The Robinhood connection** is read only with `ROBINHOOD_ACCOUNT_READ=1` and
`DATA_MODE=robinhood`.

- **Tools.** The bridge may call, by exact name, only names of the form
  `get_(equity|stock|option)_(positions|orders)`. This is enforced in
  `mcp_bridge.acceptable_account_tool_name`, and the list itself is
  `robinhood_account.ACCOUNT_TOOLS`. No balance, account number, bank or document tool fits
  that form. A listed tool the server marks destructive or not read-only is refused as well.
- **What counts as a fill.** Only filled quantity counts, from the executions, or from
  `cumulative_quantity` and `average_price`, whatever the order's state; a cancelled order can
  have fills. The ordered quantity and limit price are used only when the order's state says
  it filled.
- **Unreadable orders.** An order with no state and no fills, and an order without a time,
  side, strike or expiration, is reported with its field names, never its values.
- **Updates.** An order that filled further since the last read updates its row
  (`rows_updated`).
- **Overlap with the report.** Connection rows are left out on any day an imported report
  covers, because the report includes fees and this way nothing is counted twice. They count
  before and after those days.
- **Caching.** The connection's holdings and tool list are read at most every 5 minutes.
- **The check command.** `python run.py --robinhood-check` with account reads on records each
  reply's *shape* only:
  - field names and types;
  - a key that is a value (an account number, a ticker) replaced;
  - no list counts.

### Rebuilding trades (`tradeanalysis.rebuild`, `summarize`)

Rows replay oldest first; within a day, in the report's order reversed. A close that finds
nothing open is retried after that day's opens.

- **Lots.** Lots close first in, first out. Every piece's result comes from the cash in the
  Amount column, fees included; when a row has no amount, price × size is used and marked
  estimated.
- **Grouping.** Legs opened the same day in the same stock are one trade. A leg filled in
  several rows is merged before the strategy is named.
- **Assignment or exercise.** The option closes at no value, and the shares delivered that day
  join the same trade, which stays open until they are sold.
- **Options left hanging.** An option past expiration with no closing row is `UNRESOLVED`:
  its result is `null` and its risk ends at the expiration.
- **Missing history.** A close with nothing open (opened before the history starts) is listed
  under `unmatched` and left out of every figure.

**Risk at the open** (`classify`) is the most the trade could lose as opened:

| Strategy | Most it could lose |
| --- | --- |
| Shares | their cost |
| Long call or put, debit spread, long straddle or strangle, butterfly | the debit or premium paid |
| Credit spread | width − credit |
| Short put | strike × 100 − premium |
| Iron condor or butterfly | wider side − credit |
| Buy-write | cost − premium |
| Covered call | counted with the shares |
| Uncovered call, short shares, short straddle or strangle | `unlimited` |
| Anything else | `not measured` |

Unlimited and unmeasured risk are counted separately and never added in as zero.

### The analysis (`tradeanalysis.analyse`, `GET /v2/history/analysis`)

Query parameters: `start`, `end` (YYYY-MM-DD; leave both out for all history), `by`
(`week|month|quarter|year`), `basis` (`close|open`).

- **What the results count.**
  - `basis=close` counts trades closed in the range, with periods by close date.
  - `basis=open` counts trades opened in the range, with periods by open date and each trade's
    whole result in the period it opened.
  - Each trade in `trades` carries `in_results`.
- **What the risk counts:** every trade open at some point in the range.
- **Risk measures.**
  - Dollar-days: risk × the fraction still open, summed per calendar day; it shrinks with
    partial closes.
  - `exposure`: one entry per weekday.
  - `peak_exposure`: the busiest day, ranked by measured risk, then unlimited, then not
    measured. It is `null` when nothing was open, so a day with only unlimited risk is never
    a "$0" peak.
- **Strategies** are ranked by exposure: any unlimited first, then dollar-days, then total risk.
- **Summary figures:** a Wilson 95% range on the win rate, profit factor, average win and
  loss, and the largest fall from a high of the running total.
- **Patterns** compare each kind of closed trade with your average closed trade:
  - the edge is net − average × n;
  - only kinds with at least 5 trades are ranked;
  - a two-kind feature (after a loss or not) is listed once, on the side of its larger effect.

  The features are:

  - strategy;
  - days held;
  - days to expiration at the open;
  - how it ended;
  - where a single bought option's strike was at the open;
  - size against your median for that strategy (at least 3 trades);
  - weekday;
  - whether it was opened within 2 days of a loss;
  - how often you had traded the stock before.

  These are counts of your own past trades, not forecasts.
