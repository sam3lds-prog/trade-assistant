# Trade Assistant 4.0: a model that learns from its own frozen predictions

4.0 adds a learning engine that is judged the way it would have to earn trust in practice. It walks through ten years
of history one trading session at a time, as the app would have lived through it. It forecasts each stock from what
was knowable after that session's close, freezes the forecast, and grades it once its window has passed. It retrains
on outcomes that have matured, and it replaces a model only when a challenger has beaten it, by more than chance, on
data neither had trained on.

The question it answers is not "can a model look good on history" but "would this app, running every day since
2016, have made better forecasts and better trades than doing nothing clever, prediction by prediction".

It is delivered as three pull requests:

| PR | Phases | What it delivers | Order transmission |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1 Replay foundation, 2 Learning models, 3 Trade outcomes | The frozen dataset and calendar, the as-of view, the immutable ledger, baselines, next-day and five-day models with weekly walk-forward retraining and champion/challenger promotion, the target/stop/timeout model and take/pass gate, scorecards, the command line, read-only routes, grading of Research's live forecasts | none |
| B | 4 Holdout and shadow, 5 Model Lab | One sealed evaluation of the held-out year; daily shadow forecasts from live data; the Model Lab workspace; validated predictions in Discover and Research | none |
| C | 6 Paper trading, 7 Specialists, factors, events | Model proposals to the paper broker with attribution; per-stock corrections; factor features; structured event extraction | paper only |

The engine (`tradeassist/intelligence/`) cannot place an order. It reads market data only through a function it is
handed, and `tests/test_v4_isolation.py` fails if any of its modules can reach the Trading Desk, a broker, the
autopilot, a data provider or the app's service, checked both by following every import and by importing the package
in a fresh interpreter.

## Decisions fixed before building

These were settled in review before any code was written, and each is enforced in code, not left as a setting:

| Decision | Choice | Where it is enforced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Universe | Today's 100 large caps (`scanner.LARGE_CAPS`) plus SPY, QQQ and the 11 sector funds | `data/universe.py`; every scorecard carries the survivorship note |
| History | Development on November 2016 to October 2025; November 2025 to today held out | `config.HOLDOUT_FROM`; a development run refuses an end on or after it and does not even load later bars |
| Horizons | One and five sessions, graded only once each window has closed | `replay/labels.py`, `replay/clock.py` |
| Storage | `data/replay.db`, its own SQLite file and migrations; no new storage dependency | `intelligence/store.py` |

Defaults chosen without asking, recorded so that they can be challenged:

- Predictions are made from each session's completed daily bar, standing one hour after the close (5 p.m. New York
  time; 2 p.m. after a 1 p.m. early close).
- Prices are used as Tradier supplies them: adjusted for splits; the app records dividend adjustment as `unknown`, so
  an ex-dividend day shows as a small drop that no model can foresee. It is noted in every scorecard, not corrected.
- Gradient boosting comes from scikit-learn (`HistGradientBoostingRegressor`), the same histogram algorithm as
  LightGBM. LightGBM's macOS wheels need Homebrew's `libomp`; scikit-learn's carry their own. MLflow is not used: a
  `model_versions` table and hashed artifacts do the same job without a framework the app otherwise avoids.
- Retraining is weekly, after the last session of each week; each fit sees at most the last 1,000 sessions.

## Phase 1: the replay foundation

### The frozen dataset (`data/dataset.py`)

The app's bar cache cannot serve a replay: `Market.bars` deletes and rewrites a stock's rows on every refresh and keeps
about three years. A dataset is fetched once (`python run.py --replay dataset build`), written to `replay.db`, and
named by the hash of its content (`ds-` plus 16 hex). Reloading it recomputes the hash and refuses a file that no
longer matches. Its bars cannot be updated or deleted (database triggers).

Each dataset is checked as it is frozen. Every check is recorded and shown, and a run refuses a dataset with a
failed check unless told otherwise:

| Check | Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CALENDAR_MISSING_SESSION` / `CALENDAR_EXTRA_SESSION` | fail | SPY's trading days disagree with the exchange calendar where `marketdays.py` covers the year |
| `SUSPECT_MOVE` | warn | A one-day move over 40%: possibly a split the source did not adjust |
| `MISSING_SESSIONS` | info or warn | A stock has no bar on some sessions; it is not predicted on those days |
| `LATER_START` | info | A later listing (PLTR in 2020); it joins once it has a year of bars |
| `NO_DATA`, `FETCH_FAILED` | warn | A symbol could not be had; it is left out |
| `ADJUSTMENT_UNKNOWN` | info | The source does not say whether dividends are adjusted |

### The calendar (`data/calendar.py`)

`marketdays.py` lists holidays from 2025 only. Before that, the sessions are the days SPY traded in the dataset;
from 2025, the two must agree. Horizons count sessions, so a one-day forecast made on the Wednesday before
Thanksgiving is graded at Friday's close. This fixed a bug in 3.x as well: `forecast.record_target` counted pandas
business days, so Research's saved forecasts whose horizon crossed a holiday were dated to the holiday.

### Features that cannot see the future (`data/features.py`)

There are 22 causal features: 3.0's analog features (moves and gaps in daily ranges, volume, RSI, distance from the
50-day average, volatility and its rank, the market's trend and volatility), plus longer returns, the 200-day
distance, the market's one- and five-day returns, strength against the sector fund, and the stock's trailing drift.
Every one comes from rolling windows only, so the table is computed once and row T is read on session T.
`test_every_feature_on_a_session_is_the_same_whatever_comes_after_it` proves this equals recomputing from data that
stops at T.

### The as-of view (`replay/clock.py`)

Models, trainers and graders are handed an `AsOf` for the session, never the dataset. It gives out the session's
eligible rows, training rows whose outcomes had matured, and closing prices up to the cutoff. Asking for a later
price raises `LookaheadError`.

### Maturity (`replay/labels.py`)

The daily label of session S is the return from S's close to S+h's close, and it exists only once S+h has closed. A
trade set up on S enters at S+1's open and is held to S+5 at most; its label matures at S+5's close, even if a
barrier was reached earlier, so a session's trade labels all mature together.
`test_a_five_day_outcome_is_invisible_to_training_until_its_window_closes` checks every day from T to T+7.

### The ledger (`learning/ledger.py`)

Predictions, outcomes and take/pass decisions are inserted, never changed: triggers abort any UPDATE or DELETE.
Each prediction stores its model version, training cutoff, feature version, a hash of its feature row, the time it
stands for, and a hash of its own content. A run's ledger hash folds them together in a fixed order.

### The day loop (`replay/runner.py`)

Each session runs in one transaction:

1. Grade every prediction due.
2. Train, on the schedule.
3. Predict and freeze.
4. Checkpoint.

A run stopped anywhere, by Ctrl-C, a crash, or a sleeping laptop, resumes after its checkpoint with exactly the models
it had (`run_state` keeps each task's current artifact, checked against its hash). It ends with the same ledger hash
as an uninterrupted run (`test_the_same_data_and_settings_give_the_same_ledger_even_after_a_stop_and_resume`).

## Phase 2: models that learn, and are only promoted on evidence

### The competitors (`models/`)

| Family | Task | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `no_change` | daily | The price stays where it closed. Every run starts with it as champion |
| `drift` | daily | The stock's own average daily return over the last year, carried forward |
| `engines30` | daily | 3.0's Research forecast run daily: momentum, mean reversion and drift, ridge-weighted toward "no move". The analog search is left out; it is too slow to run for 100 stocks on every session of ten years |
| `ridge` | daily | Linear on all 22 features, in volatility units, shrunk toward zero |
| `gbm` | daily | Gradient-boosted trees on the same inputs |
| `base_rate` | trade | How often each outcome happened for the setup. Starting champion |
| `gbm_cls` | trade | Gradient-boosted classifier, probabilities calibrated on a later block |

Every daily model's 80% band comes from residuals on a block it never trained on, so its width is an out-of-sample
estimate, never an in-sample one.

### A training day (`learning/trainer.py`)

```
[ fit .............................. | band ] [ validation window ]      choosing the family
[ fit ............................................. | band ]              deploying it through the cutoff
```

Each split is purged so that no training label's window reaches into the rows after it. Every allowed family, the
champion's included, is fitted on the same early rows and judged on the same validation window. The family that
stays, whether the champion's or a promoted challenger's, is refitted on everything matured. Weekly retraining
therefore keeps the champion current even when nothing beats it.

### The promotion rule (`config.PromotionRule`, `learning/validation.py`)

The rule is fixed before any replay and is the same for every run. A daily challenger is promoted only when all of
these hold:

- **Better by more than chance.** Its error is lower than the champion's on the validation window, by more than
  chance. The test is a one-sided Student t on the per-date mean gain. It uses only dates whose windows do not
  overlap, so every fifth date for five-day forecasts. Alpha is 5% divided among the challengers tested that day.
- **Enough evidence.** The window holds at least 30 such dates: 60 sessions for one-day forecasts, 150 for five-day.
- **An honest band.** Its 80% band held 80% ± 7 points of outcomes.
- **No worse tail.** Its 95th-percentile error is no more than 5% worse.
- **Twice running.** It passed on two training days in a row.

Two of these came out of the build itself. The first synthetic replay promoted a weak five-day model repeatedly. Four
challengers tested weekly at a plain 5% make a lucky winner likely within a few months. Separately, 40 sessions of
overlapping five-day windows hold only about 8 independent observations, and a Newey-West correction is overconfident
at that size. The rule above fixed both; the five-day champion now correctly stays "no change" on data with no
five-day edge.

A trade challenger is promoted on the same pattern. Its log loss must improve by more than chance, measured on
non-overlapping blocks of five sessions. The trades it would take must not earn less after costs than the
champion's. It must also pass twice running.

## Phase 3: the narrow trade

`config.TradeSpec`, versioned by its hash, defines the trade:

- **Setup:** a pullback of at least 2.5 daily ranges from the 20-day high, above the 50- and 200-day averages
  (`drop` and `gap_down` are also available).
- **Entry:** the next session's open.
- **Target and stop:** a target 1.5 daily ranges above the entry, a stop 1 below.
- **Time limit:** five sessions.
- **Costs:** 5 basis points per side.

The target/stop/timeout model gives a probability for each outcome. The expected net return follows from them and
from what each outcome has paid on average, in daily ranges. The take/pass gate takes a trade only when that
expectation clears 0.10%, and never before a trade model has been trained.

**The ambiguity rule.** Tradier's intraday history reaches back weeks, not years, so a historical day whose range
spans both the target and the stop cannot say which came first. It is labelled `ambiguous` and scored as the stop,
the worse case. A gap through the stop fills at the open, worse than the stop. Scorecards count the ambiguous days.

Every setup gets a frozen forecast and a decision, and every one is graded. A scorecard therefore compares three
views of the same setups: taking all of them, taking the ones the gate chose, and taking the ones it passed.

## Scorecards (`replay/metrics.py`)

Daily forecasts are scored against "no change" on the same rows: the gain in absolute error per date, its Newey-West
t (with thousands of dates, the correction is reliable), band coverage, and directional accuracy when the forecast
leaned. Results are broken down by year, by market regime, and by which family was champion. Trades are scored for
win rate, expectancy, profit factor and the deepest fall of the running total, for all setups, taken, and passed.
Price accuracy and trade results are kept apart.

The headline is one sentence, for example: *"1-day forecasts beat 'no change' (+0.84 bp of error per stock-day,
t = 2.15, 5,960 graded); 5-day forecasts did not beat 'no change' …; the gate took 2 of 161 setups …"*.

## Research's own forecasts, graded at last (`v4/grading.py`)

Since 3.0, every Research study has saved its forecast to `forecast_records`, with empty columns for what happened.
Nothing ever filled them. An hourly job now grades each record once its target session's bar is final, filling in
the realized move, whether the band held, and whether the median's lean was right. The job runs in the daemon, or in
the app when no daemon runs. `GET /v4/research/record` summarises the results by horizon and model version.

## Using it

```
python run.py --replay dataset build        # about 11 years for 113 symbols from your Tradier token (a few minutes)
python run.py --replay dataset list
python run.py --replay run                  # the development replay, Nov 2016 to Oct 2025
python run.py --replay resume RUN           # after Ctrl-C, a crash or a sleep
python run.py --replay report RUN
```

**How long a replay takes.** In this build's sandbox (2 CPU cores), predicting and grading 100 stocks cost about 0.1 s
per session, and a training day at the full 1,000-session window about 3 s (one gradient-boosted fit on 100,000 rows
takes 1.3 s). The full development replay, about 2,250 sessions with 470 weekly training days, comes to roughly half an
hour there, and less on a recent Mac. `--retrain monthly` cuts training work about fourfold, and `--families ridge`
skips the trees. The replay runs in its own process, so the Trading Desk is never slowed.

## What this does not claim

- A replay on today's large caps overstates what any model would have earned, because survivorship bias flatters
  every result equally. It is fit for comparing models with each other, not for promising returns.
- Historical replay is not a forward record. Phase 4 starts that record: shadow forecasts saved before the future
  happens.
- A model that does not beat "no change" is reported as such. No headline says "trained successfully".
