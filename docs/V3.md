# Trade Assistant 3.0

3.0 turns the collection of screens into one trading workstation: **Discover**, **Research**, **Trading Desk**
and **Portfolio**, built from linked widgets that share one selected stock, contract, horizon, scenario and
proposal. One opportunity record flows from discovery to research, to a proposal, to authorisation, to an order,
to the position and its review, so a trade idea is never rebuilt by hand.

It is delivered in seven phases, each a pull request with its own tests:

| Phase | What it delivers | Order transmission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Stabilise | Reproducible CI, the P0 risk-figure fixes, versioned contracts, the decision tables, the event stream | none |
| 2 Workstation | Four workspaces of movable, resizable, linked widgets; saved layouts | none |
| 3 Research | One study per stock: peer and event analogs, a calibrated forecast, option premium history, strategies | none |
| 4 Discover | One deduplicated opportunity feed with a comparable rank; one capital allocator | none |
| 5 Trading Desk | Proposals, approvals, the risk engine, policies, notifications, the daemon, phone approvals | paper only |
| 6 Robinhood | A separate execution adapter, off by default, single-leg limit orders in the MCP account | gated, off |
| 7 Autopilot | Capped autopilot under a written policy, shadow mode, hardening | gated, off |

Nothing in 3.0 sends a real order unless it is switched on in `.env`, the Robinhood MCP account is named, the
kill switch is off and the deterministic risk engine passes; see Phase 6.

## Phase 1: stabilise and establish the contracts

### The tests run on a fixed clock

The 2.16 suite read the real clock, so its result depended on when it ran: the same commit (`341fe18`) failed two
tests at 7:18 p.m. New York and six at 2:12 a.m., and fifteen at 9:37 a.m. here. The demo market is generated
around today's date, so even the option prices moved from day to day.

`tests/conftest.py` now starts a clock before collection (`time-machine`, which patches `datetime.now`,
`time.time` and `date.today` everywhere) at **Thursday 22 October 2026, 17:00 New York**: after the close, when
every session in a chart's range is complete and the demo market has stocks that moved more than a daily range.
The clock ticks on from there, so timeouts and threads behave normally. A test that is about a particular time
of day says so with `@pytest.mark.clock("2026-10-22T10:00")` (the morning plan's day-trade cards, built in the
session; the option evaluator's chains dated 6 October).

None of the failures was a defect in the app: each test assumed the time of day it was written at. The fix is in
the tests.

### The two P0 risk-figure corrections (whatif.py)

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| `pnl_p10`/`p50`/`p90` were the P&L at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentile **prices** | They are percentiles of the **P&L** itself. A position whose P&L only rises or only falls with the price reads the same as before (exactly, at the matching or mirrored price); a straddle, a butterfly or a condor now reports its real 10th percentile, which sits where the stock does not move |
| A combination's `max_loss` was the worst point of the curve inside the scenario's ±4σ range | `max_loss` is the **theoretical maximum loss** from the combined payoff at expiration. The modeled worst loss is reported beside it and caps nothing |

Every position, combination and budget split now carries the four risk measures apart (`tradeassist/riskmath.py`):

- `theoretical_max_loss`: from the contract terms (None when unlimited, or when options expire on different days);
- `modeled_worst_loss`: the worst simulated outcome;
- `expected_shortfall`: the average loss in the worst tenth of outcomes (the 2.x `cvar10`, kept as an alias);
- `capital_required`: what the account must put up, with its rule (cash, Regulation T for a short, the maximum
  loss for a defined-risk credit; None where the broker sets the margin).

Before expiration a European model can value a deep in-the-money put a little below intrinsic, by the interest
it gives up, so the modeled worst can sit a few dollars past the contractual floor (the baseline shows $412.88
against $399.00 for 100 shares and a put). That is the model, not a looser cap.

The 2.x hedge test asserted that a protective put always improves the 10th percentile. It does not: when that
outcome is above the strike the premium makes it worse. The test now asserts what is always true: the put
lowers the theoretical maximum loss and the worst modeled loss, and costs at the 90th percentile.

### Regression baselines

`tests/test_regression_baseline.py` records Black-76 prices and Greeks, the implied-volatility solver, a
similar-pattern search, six what-if positions, a combination and a two-stock blend on the demo market at a fixed
moment (`tests/fixtures/regression_baseline.json`). Any later change that moves one fails until it is re-recorded
on purpose (`REGENERATE_BASELINE=1`).

### Versioned contracts and the decision tables

`tradeassist/contracts.py` defines the objects that pass between the workspaces (contract version `3.0`, strict:
unknown fields and non-finite numbers are refused):

```
Opportunity ── StudyRun ── EvidenceSnapshot, ForecastVersion, StrategyCandidate
                              └── TradeProposal ── RiskAssessment, Approval or policy authorisation
                                                      └── BrokerOrder ── Fill
```

- `OrderTerms.order_hash()` is the SHA-256 of the exact terms (account, symbol, side, quantity, type, limit, time
  in force, extended hours, contract). An approval authorises that hash and nothing else; a proposal whose hash
  does not match its terms cannot be built.
- `TRANSITIONS` is the proposal state machine. A submission whose outcome was not seen goes to `unknown`, and only
  reconciliation with the broker leaves `unknown`: there is no path from it back to `submitting`, so a timeout can
  never become a blind retry.

Migration 19 (`tradeassist/schema_v3.py`) adds the tables: `opportunity_candidates` (one active per stock,
direction and horizon, enforced by a partial unique index) and `opportunity_observations`, `studies`,
`forecast_records`, `trading_policies`, `trade_proposals` and `proposal_events`, `approvals`, `broker_orders`
(a unique client order id: the idempotency key) and `broker_fills`, `budget_ledger`, `jobs`, `heartbeats`,
`notifications`, `workspace_layouts`, `remote_sessions`, `execution_state`, `audit_log` and `app_events`. Every
trading table records the data mode and the account kind (paper, shadow, live), so those records never mix.

### The shared event stream

`tradeassist/bus.py` writes every change to `app_events` and wakes waiting readers. `GET /v3/events?after=N&wait=20`
returns what changed after event N, holding the reply up to 20 seconds. Because the stream is in the database,
the trading daemon (a separate process from Phase 5) and the web app read the same one; a reader in the other
process sees an event within a second. Events carry ids and a little context, never a credential.

The database connection now waits up to 15 seconds for another process's write lock instead of 5.

## Phase 2: the workstation

`http://127.0.0.1:8000/` now opens the workstation; the 2.x screens are at `/classic`, unchanged, and every one of
them can also be opened as a widget (the **Classic screens** menu).

### Four workspaces

| Workspace | Standard layout | Its job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | Opportunities, watchlist, news, chart, the Morning plan | Where are the best risk-adjusted trades right now, and why? |
| Research | Chart, options chain, scenario explorer, strategy builder, risk / reward, news | Everything about one stock, from history to an order-ready contract |
| Trading Desk | Watchlist, activity, paper trading (the desk proper arrives in phase 5) | Proposals, approvals and orders |
| Portfolio | Positions, portfolio risk, journal, my trades | What is held, what it risks, how it went |

Each workspace is a tab. **Layout → New …** opens another of the same kind (two Research tabs side by side), and
**Layout → Save as** keeps a named copy that can be reopened later or made the workspace's default.

### Widgets

Every widget can be moved (drag its header), resized (its edges), collapsed, maximized (Escape restores), closed,
pinned to the dock, and added from **Add widget** or the command palette.

| Widget | What it shows | Updates its link with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stock chart | The interactive chart: ranges, groupings, indicators, swings, patterns, outcomes | the stock it shows |
| Scenario explorer | The what-if model, drawn on the chart in the same link | — |
| Options chain | Bid, ask, implied volatility and delta by strike; calls, puts, both, or the side the trade leans | the contract clicked |
| Strategy builder | Any of the sixteen structures on real listed contracts | the strategy (and a single leg as the contract) |
| Risk / reward | The selected contract's or strategy's P&L at the horizon and at expiry, with an IV slider | — |
| Order ticket | The fair price, the limit ladder and the checks for the selected contract | — |
| Opportunities | The ranked feed of every scan (the one deduplicated feed arrives in phase 4) | the stock, direction and horizon clicked |
| Watchlist | Last and change for your tickers | the stock clicked |
| News & events | Headlines and filings, with their tone | — |
| Activity | Everything the app and (from phase 5) the daemon change, live | — |
| Classic screens | Any 2.x screen inside a widget (one copy of each at a time) | "Analyze" opens Research on that stock |

### Links

A widget's link colour (Blue, Green, Amber, Plum, or Not linked) is the coloured stripe down its header. Widgets of
one colour share one context: the stock, the direction, the horizon, the contract, the strategy, the scenario and
the draft proposal. Picking a stock in any of them, or typing one in the palette (Ctrl K / Cmd K), updates them
all, in every workspace; a new stock clears the old stock's contract and strategy. Two charts on two colours
show two stocks side by side. The palette sends a ticker to the link of the widget last clicked.

### The dock and the status strip

The dock on the right keeps one widget in view whichever workspace is open (the order ticket by default). The
status strip under the tabs always shows the data source and whether prices are real, delayed or made up, the
market's state, the trading mode (advisory until phase 5), and whether live updates are flowing.

### What is saved

Everything, as it changes: the open workspaces and their widgets' places, sizes, links and settings, each link's
context (so a half-built idea survives a restart), and the dock. A phone shows one column in reading order and
still saves the twelve-column layout.

### Engineering notes

- GridStack.js 12.3.3 (MIT) is vendored next to Lightweight Charts; no CDN, no build step, no React.
- The workstation loads the classic scripts and runs their renderers inside widgets. Four small changes make
  that possible: `show()` hands a screen to the workstation when it sets `state.route`; `loadStatus()` does not
  need the classic status box; the classic page alone runs `start()`; and a chart no longer disposes every other
  chart (the Analyze screen disposes its own), so several charts can live on one page.
- Everything new is under one global, `WS` (a test keeps it that way).
- `/v3/workspaces` (list, get, save, delete), `/v3/workspaces/session`, `/v3/symbols` and `/v3/quotes`.
