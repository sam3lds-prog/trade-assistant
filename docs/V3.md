# Trade Assistant 3.0

3.0 turns the collection of screens into one trading workstation: **Discover**, **Research**, **Trading Desk**
and **Portfolio**, built from linked widgets that share one selected stock, contract, horizon, scenario and
proposal. One opportunity record flows from discovery to research, to a proposal, to authorisation, to an order,
to the position and its review, so a trade idea is never rebuilt by hand.

It is delivered in seven phases, each a pull request with its own tests:

| Phase | What it delivers | Order transmission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Stabilise | Reproducible CI, the P0 risk-figure fixes, versioned contracts, the decision tables, the event stream | none |
| 2 Workstation | Four workspaces of movable, resizable, linked widgets; saved layouts | none |
| 3 Research | One study per stock: peer and event analogs, a calibrated forecast, option premium history, strategies | none |
| 4 Discover | One deduplicated opportunity feed with a comparable rank; one capital allocator | none |
| 5 Trading Desk | Proposals, approvals, the risk engine, policies, notifications, the daemon, phone approvals | paper only |
| 6 Robinhood | A separate execution adapter, off by default, single-leg limit orders in the MCP account | gated, off |
| 7 Autopilot | Capped autopilot under a written policy, shadow mode, hardening | gated, off |

Nothing in 3.0 sends a real order unless it is switched on in `.env`, the Robinhood MCP account is named, the
kill switch is off and the deterministic risk engine passes; see Phase 6.

## Phase 1: stabilise and establish the contracts

### The tests run on a fixed clock

The 2.16 suite read the real clock, so its result depended on when it ran: the same commit (`341fe18`) failed two
tests at 7:18 p.m. New York and six at 2:12 a.m., and fifteen at 9:37 a.m. here. The demo market is generated
around today's date, so even the option prices moved from day to day.

`tests/conftest.py` now starts a clock before collection (`time-machine`, which patches `datetime.now`,
`time.time` and `date.today` everywhere) at **Thursday 22 October 2026, 17:00 New York**: after the close, when
every session in a chart's range is complete and the demo market has stocks that moved more than a daily range.
The clock ticks on from there, so timeouts and threads behave normally. A test that is about a particular time
of day says so with `@pytest.mark.clock("2026-10-22T10:00")` (the morning plan's day-trade cards, built in the
session; the option evaluator's chains dated 6 October).

None of the failures was a defect in the app: each test assumed the time of day it was written at. The fix is in
the tests.

### The two P0 risk-figure corrections (whatif.py)

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| `pnl_p10`/`p50`/`p90` were the P&L at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentile **prices** | They are percentiles of the **P&L** itself. A position whose P&L only rises or only falls with the price reads the same as before (exactly, at the matching or mirrored price); a straddle, a butterfly or a condor now reports its real 10th percentile, which sits where the stock does not move |
| A combination's `max_loss` was the worst point of the curve inside the scenario's ±4σ range | `max_loss` is the **theoretical maximum loss** from the combined payoff at expiration. The modeled worst loss is reported beside it and caps nothing |

Every position, combination and budget split now carries the four risk measures apart (`tradeassist/riskmath.py`):

- `theoretical_max_loss`: from the contract terms (None when unlimited, or when options expire on different days);
- `modeled_worst_loss`: the worst simulated outcome;
- `expected_shortfall`: the average loss in the worst tenth of outcomes (the 2.x `cvar10`, kept as an alias);
- `capital_required`: what the account must put up, with its rule (cash, Regulation T for a short, the maximum
  loss for a defined-risk credit; None where the broker sets the margin).

Before expiration a European model can value a deep in-the-money put a little below intrinsic, by the interest
it gives up, so the modeled worst can sit a few dollars past the contractual floor (the baseline shows $412.88
against $399.00 for 100 shares and a put). That is the model, not a looser cap.

The 2.x hedge test asserted that a protective put always improves the 10th percentile. It does not: when that
outcome is above the strike the premium makes it worse. The test now asserts what is always true: the put
lowers the theoretical maximum loss and the worst modeled loss, and costs at the 90th percentile.

### Regression baselines

`tests/test_regression_baseline.py` records Black-76 prices and Greeks, the implied-volatility solver, a
similar-pattern search, six what-if positions, a combination and a two-stock blend on the demo market at a fixed
moment (`tests/fixtures/regression_baseline.json`). Any later change that moves one fails until it is re-recorded
on purpose (`REGENERATE_BASELINE=1`).

### Versioned contracts and the decision tables

`tradeassist/contracts.py` defines the objects that pass between the workspaces (contract version `3.0`, strict:
unknown fields and non-finite numbers are refused):

```
Opportunity ── StudyRun ── EvidenceSnapshot, ForecastVersion, StrategyCandidate
                              └── TradeProposal ── RiskAssessment, Approval or policy authorisation
                                                      └── BrokerOrder ── Fill
```

- `OrderTerms.order_hash()` is the SHA-256 of the exact terms (account, symbol, side, quantity, type, limit, time
  in force, extended hours, contract). An approval authorises that hash and nothing else; a proposal whose hash
  does not match its terms cannot be built.
- `TRANSITIONS` is the proposal state machine. A submission whose outcome was not seen goes to `unknown`, and only
  reconciliation with the broker leaves `unknown`: there is no path from it back to `submitting`, so a timeout can
  never become a blind retry.

Migration 19 (`tradeassist/schema_v3.py`) adds the tables: `opportunity_candidates` (one active per stock,
direction and horizon, enforced by a partial unique index) and `opportunity_observations`, `studies`,
`forecast_records`, `trading_policies`, `trade_proposals` and `proposal_events`, `approvals`, `broker_orders`
(a unique client order id: the idempotency key) and `broker_fills`, `budget_ledger`, `jobs`, `heartbeats`,
`notifications`, `workspace_layouts`, `remote_sessions`, `execution_state`, `audit_log` and `app_events`. Every
trading table records the data mode and the account kind (paper, shadow, live), so those records never mix.

### The shared event stream

`tradeassist/bus.py` writes every change to `app_events` and wakes waiting readers. `GET /v3/events?after=N&wait=20`
returns what changed after event N, holding the reply up to 20 seconds. Because the stream is in the database,
the trading daemon (a separate process from Phase 5) and the web app read the same one; a reader in the other
process sees an event within a second. Events carry ids and a little context, never a credential.

The database connection now waits up to 15 seconds for another process's write lock instead of 5.

## Phase 2: the workstation

`http://127.0.0.1:8000/` now opens the workstation; the 2.x screens are at `/classic`, unchanged, and every one of
them can also be opened as a widget (the **Classic screens** menu).

### Four workspaces

| Workspace | Standard layout | Its job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | Opportunities, watchlist, news, chart, the Morning plan | Where are the best risk-adjusted trades right now, and why? |
| Research | Chart, options chain, scenario explorer, strategy builder, risk / reward, news | Everything about one stock, from history to an order-ready contract |
| Trading Desk | Watchlist, activity, paper trading (the desk proper arrives in phase 5) | Proposals, approvals and orders |
| Portfolio | Positions, portfolio risk, journal, my trades | What is held, what it risks, how it went |

Each workspace is a tab. **Layout → New …** opens another of the same kind (two Research tabs side by side), and
**Layout → Save as** keeps a named copy that can be reopened later or made the workspace's default.

### Widgets

Every widget can be moved (drag its header), resized (its edges), collapsed, maximized (Escape restores), closed,
pinned to the dock, and added from **Add widget** or the command palette.

| Widget | What it shows | Updates its link with |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Opportunities | The ranked feed of every scan (the one deduplicated feed arrives in phase 4) | the stock, direction and horizon clicked |

| News & events | Headlines and filings, with their tone | — |
| Activity | Everything the app and (from phase 5) the daemon change, live | — |
| Classic screens | Any 2.x screen inside a widget (one copy of each at a time) | "Analyze" opens Research on that stock |

### Links

A widget's link colour (Blue, Green, Amber, Plum, or Not linked) is the coloured stripe down its header. Widgets of
one colour share one context: the stock, the direction, the horizon, the contract, the strategy, the scenario and
the draft proposal. Picking a stock in any of them, or typing one in the palette (Ctrl K / Cmd K), updates them
all, in every workspace; a new stock clears the old stock's contract and strategy. Two charts on two colours
show two stocks side by side. The palette sends a ticker to the link of the widget last clicked.

### The dock and the status strip

The dock on the right keeps one widget in view whichever workspace is open (the order ticket by default). The
status strip under the tabs always shows the data source and whether prices are real, delayed or made up, the
market's state, the trading mode (advisory until phase 5), and whether live updates are flowing.

### What is saved

Everything, as it changes: the open workspaces and their widgets' places, sizes, links and settings, each link's
context (so a half-built idea survives a restart), and the dock. A phone shows one column in reading order and
still saves the twelve-column layout.

## Phase 3: Research

The **Research study** widget runs one complete investigation of the linked stock: choose the horizon (1, 3, 5,
10 or 20 trading days), the kind of trade (intraday bounce, swing, earnings reaction, reversal, longer-term),
the direction and any peers to add, and press **Run the study**. It runs in the background and is saved; the link
remembers it, so it is still there after a restart.

### What a study gathers

| Evidence | From |
| --- | --- |
| Similar past situations | `analogs.py`: the stock's and its peers' history (below) |
| This stock against its peers | the same analogs, split by source |
| What options price | 30-day implied volatility, IV rank, skew, the move priced for an earnings release |
| Trend, regime, signals | the 2.x trend engine, regime and signal history |
| News | the week's headlines and their tone |

Each item says whether it supports the trade's direction, contradicts it, or is context. Anything that could not
be read is listed under **Missing or not yet trusted**, never filled in. The record keeps the as-of time, the data
source, every engine's version and a fingerprint of the inputs.

### Analogs that never see the future (`analogs.py`)

The situation is the latest event (a gap of a daily range or more, a move of two daily ranges, a volume spike of
2.5 times average, a pullback in an uptrend, a breakout to a 60-day high on volume, an earnings day) or, without
one, the setup itself. Candidates are matched on nine causal features (the move, the gap, volume, RSI, distance
from the 50-day average, the 20-day return, volatility against its year, the drop from the 20-day high, the
market's 20-day return). Guards, each with a test:

- every feature uses only its own day and earlier ones;
- a candidate counts only when its whole forward window ended before the study's day, and the stock's own last
  days, whose windows would reach today, are never candidates;
- analogs that overlap on one stock, or fall on the same day on two stocks, are marked dependent, weighted half,
  and left out of the frequencies (which use the independent count, with a Wilson interval).

Peers (`peers.py`) are the stock's sector names whose daily returns moved most closely with it over the last year,
plus the ones you add, minus the ones you remove.

### One forecast, learned and judged on history (`forecast.py`)

The base path combines four engines' readings: the analogs' median, the last 20 days carried forward, the pull
back toward the 50-day average, and the stock's own drift. Their weights come from **Run the calibration** in the
**Forecast record** widget:

1. Every fifth day over several years, on the watchlist's stocks, each engine forecasts from data up to that day.
2. The earlier 70% of dates fit the weights (ridge regression shrunk toward "no move") and the band's width (so that
   80% of outcomes fell inside the 80% band).
3. The later 30%, never seen, judge them: how often the band held, how far the median missed against assuming
   no move, how often it had the direction right, each by market regime.
4. The median must beat "no move" with one-sided 95% confidence (per date, since one day across many stocks is
   one event). If it does not, the weights are zero and the base path is flat.

**Calibrated** means the band held within seven points of 80% on the unseen dates. Until the calibration has run
the forecast says **Not calibrated**, gives no weight to any engine, and draws a flat path with the volatility's
band. On the demo market (a random walk) the calibration finds no direction, as it should, and says so.

The band's width blends realised and implied volatility, and an earnings release inside the horizon adds the move
options price for it from that day. **Other paths to test against** are labelled assumptions: an earnings beat and a
guidance cut (by the priced move), a market rally and sell-off (a one-in-ten market move times the stock's beta).
No probability is ever attached to them. **What would prove it wrong** names the closes at the bottom of the band,
the release, and a change of regime. **Draw on the linked chart** draws the base, its band and the alternatives.

### The contract's own price history (`premium.py`, the Option chart widget)

Selecting a contract shows its own daily bars: from Tradier's history for its OCC symbol, or from Robinhood's
`get_option_historicals` (a read-only tool added to the allowlist; the test of the exact list changed with it on
purpose). Without either, the chart shows the contract's **theoretical** value on each day (Black-76 from that day's
close and today's implied volatility), dashed, tagged "Theoretical, not traded", with no open, high or low.

### Strategies under the same scenarios (`studio.py`)

Every candidate for the direction (shares or a short, a call or put, the vertical spreads; a straddle and a condor
when earnings fall inside the horizon) is valued under the base path and each alternative, after round-trip costs
(STOCK_COST_BPS each way for shares; the quoted spread for options). For each: the percentiles of its P&L, the four
risk measures, the breakevens, what five points of implied volatility up or down does, and before earnings the
volatility collapse after the release (a long option can lose with the stock moving the right way). An estimated
fill chance comes from the quoted spread. Only shares and single options are marked as ones the Trading Desk can
send; spreads stay research and hand-entered orders. The best by expected result per dollar of maximum loss is
marked, or the comparison says to hold cash when nothing clears its costs. Clicking a row sends it to the linked
ticket, risk panel and option chart.

### The AI's part

**Ask the AI to challenge it** sends the study's evidence (not the figures to change) to Claude or ChatGPT,
whichever is set up, and gets back what supports the thesis, what contradicts it, what is missing, what would prove
it wrong and the questions to answer first, in words only. In the scenario explorer the AI now chooses settings and
explains them, and the path drawn is the app's own model under those settings; the AI's own figure is named, not
drawn.

### Routes

`POST /v3/studies`, `GET /v3/studies[?symbol=]`, `GET /v3/studies/{id}`, `POST /v3/studies/{id}/challenge`,
`GET /v3/peers/{symbol}`, `GET /v3/forecast/calibration`, `POST /v3/forecast/calibrate`, `GET /v3/options/history`.
Migration 20 adds `studies.view_json` (what the screen shows beside the contract).

## Phase 4: Discover

2.x had five lists (the Morning plan, Trade ideas, Live news, the Scanner, Reversals), each scored its own way. The
hub sorted their scores together as if they were comparable, and a stock that three of them flagged appeared three
times. 3.0 keeps the scanners and changes what they produce.

### One candidate per trade (`v3/discover.py`)

Each scanner's finding is recorded as an **observation** (`opportunity_observations`, one row per scanner and
finding; seen again, it is refreshed, not added). Observations of the same stock, direction and horizon merge into
one **candidate** (`opportunity_candidates`; a partial unique index allows one active candidate per stock,
direction and horizon in each data mode). The candidate lists every scanner that found it and every kind of setup
they saw, and keeps the clearest levels (entry, invalidation, target) any of them gave.

A swing candidate lives three trading days past its newest sighting, an intraday one until the close. When it
expires, its observations stay with it: a later sighting starts a new candidate with only what was seen since.

### A rank on one scale, with its parts shown

| part | weight | what it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical support | 0.35 | the after-cost record of the same trade, discounted by cases / (cases + 50) |
| Setup quality | 0.25 | the best scanner score among the sightings, on that scanner's own 0-100 scale |
| Catalyst | 0.15 | news behind it now (1), an earnings release inside the horizon (0.6) |
| Liquidity | 0.15 | the stock's average dollar volume, $1M to $1B on a log scale |
| Freshness | 0.10 | the newest sighting's age against the candidate's life |
| Execution cost | -0.10 | the quoted spread (50 basis points or more counts fully) |
| Conflict | -0.15 | an active candidate the opposite way on the same stock and horizon |

The score is the weighted sum over the positive weights' total, from 0 to 100. Each part carries a sentence saying
what it found; the feed shows them as bars with their points. The scanner signals' record is read the way Trade
Ideas reads it (2.7): the mean move against SPY ten days on, in the trade's direction, scaled to a five-day swing,
shrunk by independent periods, less costs.

### What the autopilot may consider

A candidate is eligible only with a **validated edge** (30 or more past cases, positive after costs), no conflict,
at least $20M traded a day, and real prices (never demo). Every reason it is not is listed. It can still rank,
be studied and be traded by hand. Eligibility is necessary, not sufficient: phase 7's policy adds its own gates.

### One allocator (`allocator.py`)

`allocate_candidates` (across stocks, by the `blend.py` engine on correlated paths) and `allocate_strategies` (one
stock, by the what-if engine) answer in one shape: rows, cash kept, the combined expected result and its worst
tenth, and the most the whole split can lose from the contract terms (no limit when it includes a short). Both take
off first the capital the Trading Desk's budget ledger holds for open orders and approved proposals.

### The workstation

The **Opportunities** widget replaces the 2.x feed: filters (horizon, direction, autopilot-eligible only), Refresh
the scans (Morning plan, Trade ideas, Live news and Reversals) and Scan the watchlist too (adds the technical
scanner). Picking a row sets the link group's stock, direction, horizon and opportunity and shows how its score is
made, its levels, the autopilot's reasons and what found it. **Study it** starts a Research study bound to the
opportunity (1 trading day for an intraday one, 5 otherwise; a reversal thesis for a reversal); the study is
recorded on the candidate, and Research's study widget follows it. The **Budget split** widget splits a budget
across the top of the feed. Reading the feed takes in what the Morning plan, Trade ideas and Live news have built
since the last look (at most every two minutes; they keep their lists in memory, so nothing is rescanned).

### Routes

`GET /v3/discover[?horizon=&direction=&kind=&min_score=&eligible_only=]`, `POST /v3/discover/refresh`,
`GET /v3/discover/{id}`, `POST /v3/discover/allocate`. No migration: the tables came with migration 19.

## Phase 5: the Trading Desk

Proposals, approvals, a deterministic risk engine, a written policy, a budget ledger, notifications, a separate
daemon with a durable job queue, and phone approvals. Orders go to the **paper broker** only; live execution is
phase 6 and stays off unless it is enabled in `.env`.

### The policy (`policy.py`)

One policy is active (`trading_policies`). Its defaults are the trial: **$500** of capital, **$50** an order,
**$100** of new buying a day, a **$25** daily loss stop, **3** positions, long shares only (buy to open, sell to
close), limit orders only, regular hours from 09:35 to 15:50 New York, quotes no older than 20 seconds, spreads under
50 basis points, a limit no more than 10 basis points through the quote, 10 minutes to approve, proposals that live
an hour. It is edited in the Trading policy widget; every change is a new version and an `audit_log` row. Modes:
`disabled` (nothing is sent), `paper`, `approval_required` (live, phase 6), `capped_autopilot` (phase 7). A live
policy is refused until live execution is enabled.

### One state machine (`v3/trading.py`)

`awaiting_approval → authorized → submitting → submitted → filled`, with `blocked`, `cancelled`, `expired`,
`rejected`, `failed`, and `unknown`, which only reconciliation leaves. Every move is a `proposal_events` row with
who made it and why. A proposal is checked by the risk engine when it is written, when it is approved and just
before it is sent.

### Approvals

An approval records the hash of the exact order terms (account, stock, side, quantity, type, limit, time in force).
Approving sends the hash the person saw; a different hash, an amended proposal, a used or expired approval are all
refused. Amending supersedes every open approval. In the app, approving needs the app (it answers on this computer
only). From a phone it needs the link from the notification (a single-use token, stored as its SHA-256) and the PIN.

### The risk engine (`riskengine.py`)

Deterministic checks on numbers the desk can read: the kill switch, the mode, the account, the instrument, side,
order type and hours the policy allows, the stock lists, holding what is sold (no short sales), no duplicate open
order, a fresh two-sided quote, the spread, the limit against the quote, the order's size, capital in use, today's
new buying, the number of positions, today's result against the loss stop, the trading window, and the most the
order can lose. A check that **cannot be measured is `unknown` and blocks**: 2.x's INCOMPLETE_RISK_POLICY=WARN does
not apply to the desk. A sale of shares held is not stopped by the loss stop or an unmeasured result, so a position
can always be closed.

### Execution, reconciliation and the ledger

`desk.submit` writes the broker order with its client order id (`ta-<proposal>-v<version>`, the idempotency key)
before sending. A broker timeout or a crash leaves it `unknown`: it is never sent again, and `desk.reconcile` asks the
broker what became of that id (never received → failed, its capital released; filled → filled). Fills are recorded
once each (by the broker's fill id). The paper broker fills a buy at the ask when the ask is at or under the limit,
a sale at the bid, and expires a day order at the close. `budget_ledger` reserves an approved buy's cost, releases it
as it fills or ends, and records each sale's realized result; capital in use is reservations plus the cost of what
is held.

### The kill switch

On: working orders are cancelled, every approval is refused and every risk check fails, until it is switched off on
this computer (a phone can switch it on, not off). Each change is audited and notified.

### Notifications (`notify.py`)

An outbox (`notifications`) delivered by a job to email, ntfy and Telegram, each set up in `.env`. A failing channel
is retried alone (30 s, 2, 8, 30 minutes) and then given up; secrets never reach the record. Sent: approvals
waiting (with the phone link when phone approvals are on), blocks, fills, rejections, unknown orders (critical), the
kill switch, the daily loss stop, locked phone pages.

### The daemon and the job queue (`daemon.py`, `jobs.py`)

`python run.py --daemon`, kept running by `./setup_daemon.sh` (launchd: at login, restarted if it stops). It runs
the desk's jobs, values paper portfolios, refreshes Discover's reversal scan and scanner every 30 minutes in the
session, prunes old jobs, and serves the phone pages. Jobs are rows with a lease; a job whose worker died is queued
again. Timed jobs are queued one slot at a time with a short life, so after a sleep only the current slot runs and
missed ones are discarded. While the daemon's heartbeat is fresh the web app's own worker steps aside
(DESK_WORKER=auto) and the app's clockwork leaves the paper portfolios to it; the morning plan and the news digest
stay with the web app, whose memory they live in.

### Phone approvals (`remote.py`)

With REMOTE_APPROVALS=1 the daemon serves a few pages on REMOTE_BIND (the Tailscale address) answering only to
REMOTE_HOSTS: what is waiting, one approval with its exact terms and checks, approve or decline, send me a new link,
and switch the kill switch on. A PIN opens a 12-hour session (an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie); five wrong PINs
lock the pages for 15 minutes and send a critical notification. No scripts run there; tokens never leave in a
Referer; nothing is cached; a form from another site is refused.

To set it up: install Tailscale on the Mac and the phone; `tailscale ip -4` gives REMOTE_BIND; the Mac's MagicDNS
name goes in REMOTE_HOSTS and REMOTE_BASE_URL (`http://<name>:8765`); choose a REMOTE_PIN; run `./setup_daemon.sh`.

### The workstation

The Trading Desk opens on **Approvals** (the queue), **Proposals** (write one for the linked stock, check it, follow
it: its checks, the broker's answer and every step), **Desk** (the policy in force, capital and buying meters,
today's result, the kill switch, who runs the jobs, notifications), **Desk positions** (with "Propose selling"),
**Activity** and **Trading policy**. The status strip shows the trading mode, the risk state (kill switch, orders in
an unknown state, approvals waiting) and whether the daemon runs.

### Routes

`GET /v3/desk/status`, `GET|PUT /v3/desk/policy`, `POST /v3/desk/kill`, `GET /v3/desk/positions`,
`GET /v3/desk/ledger`, `GET /v3/desk/jobs`, `POST /v3/desk/reconcile`, `GET|POST /v3/proposals`,
`POST /v3/proposals/preview`, `GET /v3/proposals/{id}`, `POST /v3/proposals/{id}/amend|recheck|cancel`,
`POST /v3/approvals/{id}/approve|decline`, `GET /v3/notifications`, `POST /v3/notifications/test`. No path contains
"order", and every write is listed in `tests/test_api.py`. No migration: the tables came with migration 19.

## Phase 6: live execution through Robinhood

A separate adapter (`robinhood_exec.py`) sends approved share orders to Robinhood's MCP server. It is **off** unless
switched on, and the market-data connection stays read-only: that bridge still refuses any tool whose name could act.

### What it may call

Its own connection (`TradingBridge`) may call only these tools, by exact name: `get_accounts`; `review_equity_order`
or `review_stock_order`; `place_equity_order` or `place_stock_order`; `get_equity_order(s)` or
`get_stock_order(s)`; `cancel_equity_order` or `cancel_stock_order`. Names with option, crypto, transfer, withdraw,
deposit, bank, margin or exercise in them can never be put on its list, and a read the server marks as changing
something is refused. Only share limit orders, buy or sell, single leg, are sent; an option or multi-leg order is
placed by hand.

### The gates, all of them

1. `ROBINHOOD_EXECUTION=1` and `ROBINHOOD_EXECUTION_ACCOUNT` (the account's number) in `.env`.
2. The account is on the server's account list for this sign-in, and not marked as closed to agent trading.
3. The server offers the place, list and cancel tools, with arguments the adapter can fill.
4. The prices are real, not demo.
5. You set the trading policy to *Live, each order approved by you* (refused until 1 to 4 hold; the Desk widget's
   **Check live trading** runs them, and the daemon re-runs them every 10 minutes).
6. Every order passes the risk engine, including `LIVE_MAX_ORDER` (default $100), a ceiling on each live order that
   no policy can raise, and the account binding: the account is part of the order's hash, so an approval given for
   one account cannot send to another.
7. The kill switch is off.

### One attempt per order

The order carries a reference derived from the desk's client order id (a UUID, as Robinhood's `ref_id`), so the
server can recognise a repeat. When the server has a review tool, the order is reviewed first; a refusal stops it
before anything is placed. A place call that gets no answer, or whose connection drops, is **never repeated**: the
bridge's usual reconnect-and-retry is off for calls that act. The order is `unknown` until reconciliation lists the
account's orders and finds it by its reference (or, if the server does not echo references, by stock, side,
quantity, limit and time), or establishes that it never arrived (failed, capital released). Fills are taken from the
order's executions, each once; the kill switch cancels working orders at Robinhood.

### Tested against a stand-in

Robinhood does not publish these tools' arguments or replies. `tests/robinhood_trading_standin.py` follows the
shapes of Robinhood's own API and can refuse a review, never answer, or answer only after placing. Passing those
tests shows the adapter's logic; the first real use should be one share of an inexpensive stock with the policy's
smallest limits, watched on the Trading Desk.
